## What is proved

Notation: codec′ is `PqxdhBoundaryRecordPrelude.withReader codec reader` (the abstract codec with its frame reader replaced by the production decoder `RawCtxFrameDecoding.decode reader.model`), route′ is `RawCtxFrameDecoding.sender reader.model` (the production header router), "the classical source game" is `PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.sourceGame setup codec reader erased checks ensureInit 0 bounds mode adversary` and "the LQ-F source game" is `PqxdhBoundaryDeploymentLater.laterSourceGame setup codec reader erased checks ensureInit 0 bounds mode first second`, both at the Server identity index 0 that every CE-12 headline uses. The PB-D modules prove that these source games, which run the Lean source programs transcribed from the Rust adapter functions, are equal to the CE and LQ-F games at codec′ and route′ on their full returned value up to the Rust success wrapper (`classical`, `later`, `classical_zero`, `later_zero`, ledger rows PB-01 to PB-14).

`PqxdhDeploymentProductionTransfer` (8 theorems) holds the generic part. `observe_zero` and `evalDist_zero` state, for every observation of the CE game's returned value, that the observation read back through `PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.unwrap` on the classical source game is the same computation (and has the same output distribution) as the observation on `PqxdhDeploymentSecrecy.game setup codec′ checks route′ ensureInit 0 bounds mode adversary` (through `classical_zero`, `unwrap_okMap` and `classical_evalDist`); `chance` is the Boolean-event form `𝒟[(decision ∘ unwrap) <$> source] {true} = 𝒟[decision <$> game] {true}`. `observe_later_zero`, `evalDist_later_zero` and `chance_later` are the same for the LQ-F game `PqxdhDeploymentLaterServerGame.game … first second` and the LQ-F source game (through `later_zero` and `later_evalDist`). `frameReadBack_withReader` and `readWritable_withReader` state what the two codec contracts that some headlines take say at codec′ (by `Iff.rfl`); they are readings of carried premises, not discharges. `unwrap` reads the Rust `fail` and `div` results as `none`; the source games never produce them (`classical_normal`, `later_normal`).

The three headline modules restate frozen CE-12 theorems, keeping each frozen name. For a frozen theorem with binders `Bs` and a conclusion that mentions the CE (or LQ-F) game, the production form keeps `Bs` in order, adds `(reader : RawCtxNativeFramePrelude.Reader Message Root)` and `(erased : Aeneas.Std.Array Aeneas.Std.U8 229#usize)` after `codec`, drops `route`, keeps `Phase1ReadBack codec` and `Phase2ReadBack codec`, and puts codec′ for `codec` and route′ for `route` everywhere else; every block `𝒟[F <$> (PqxdhDeploymentSecrecy.game setup codec checks route ensureInit 0 bounds mode A).run runtime]` of the conclusion becomes `𝒟[(F′ ∘ PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.unwrap) <$> (PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.sourceGame setup codec reader erased checks ensureInit 0 bounds mode A).run runtime]`, with `F′` the frozen event at codec′ and route′ (LQ-F blocks likewise with `laterSourceGame` and `PqxdhBoundaryDeploymentLater.unwrap`), and the rest of the conclusion (losses, ideal games, record worlds, explicit terms, bills) is the frozen text at codec′ and route′. For `ct_collision_game`, `ct_coll_le` and `ct_coll_billed`, whose left side is the explicit term `ctCollTerm`, that term is first unfolded to its definition (the probability of `ctCollEvent` on the CE game) and then moved. Each proof is `simp only [PqxdhDeploymentProductionTransfer.chance]` (or `chance_later`) followed by the frozen theorem applied at codec′ and route′, with the read-back laws transported by `PqxdhBoundaryDeploymentStep.phase1_withReader` and `phase2_withReader` (`Iff.rfl`). So each production form has the frozen bound word for word at the production instance, and every probability of the real experiment in it is a probability of the production source game. Every other binder is a parameter as in the frozen theorem (filters, claims, guesses, targets, fallbacks of the reductions, caps). A mechanical check of every binder of the 129 forms finds no other premise that mentions `codec`, `route`, `reader` or `erased`. No premise of a selected CE-12 headline needed anything beyond existing contracts at codec′, so no owner-level stop arose.

At codec′ only the frame reader is the production decoder `RawCtxFrameDecoding.decode reader.model`; the frame writer `codec.frameWrite` and the registration codec stay abstract. So `FrameReadBack codec′` (every frame that the abstract writer writes comes out as nonempty bytes from which the production decoder returns that frame) and `ReadWritable codec′.frameWrite codec′.frameRead` (the abstract writer writes every frame that the production decoder returns) are joint conditions on the abstract writer and the reader (`frameReadBack_withReader`, `readWritable_withReader`), not laws of the abstract codec alone. They can hold together, so the 42 forms that take both are not vacuous: a writer that writes every frame which the decoder returns for some nonempty input as one such input, and writes no other frame, satisfies both whenever every frame that the decoder returns for some input it also returns for a nonempty one (for example when the decoder returns nothing for the empty input); without that condition no writer satisfies both. This follows from the two definitions and is not stated as a Lean lemma. The Phase read-back laws concern the registration writers and readers (`write1`, `read1`, `write2`, `read2` and their getters), not the frame writer, so they do not interact with it.

## What the production forms say, and what they do not

Every explicit probability of the real experiment (each `𝒟[…]` block over the CE or LQ-F game) in a production statement is now the probability of the same event on the production source game, and everything else is the frozen text at the production codec and route. In the one-sided per-target and summed forms this is the left side (and, in `beacon_gap_le`, also the CE-A1 probability on the right); in the two-sided forms (`ce_kt_close`, `ce_kt_sum`, the World close and sum forms and their later analogues) the left side is the distance between a source-game probability and a probability of CE's ideal game or record world, the LOR forms bound advantages built from several source-game probabilities, and the billed forms are conjunctions of such a bound with the bills. The bound is the frozen CE-12 bound at the production codec and route. The bound's terms are CE-model quantities at that instance: advantages of the reductions that CE-12 builds (KEM, key-schedule, record-lift, signature and collision reductions), probabilities of CE's ideal games and record worlds, the anomaly term, and the bills of those reductions. The explicit experiment terms `ctGapTerm` and `ctCollTerm` are probabilities of the production source game as well (`ctGapTerm_source`, `ctCollTerm_source`). The production composition moves only the probabilities of the real experiment; it does not re-derive any bound.

The source programs that the source games run are reviewed transcriptions of the Rust adapter (RT in the ledger); the extracted core that they call is the Aeneas extraction. The equality of the source games with the CE and LQ-F games is proved (PB-D), but the step from Rust to the source programs is review, and every RT and E entry of the ledger stays as recorded there. The composition is fixed at codec′ and route′ (lane decision D-2): a headline at another codec would need a new reader-agreement fact, which is an owner-level decision. The abstract codec, its read-back laws and the shared frame reader stay E (Cap'n Proto is out of scope); `reader` and `erased` are universally quantified parameters of the production forms, subject to `FrameReadBack codec′` and `ReadWritable codec′.frameWrite codec′.frameRead` where the form takes them (both constrain the reader together with the abstract frame writer, see "Premises and contracts carried").

Not claimed: anything about the persistence-wrapped Server surface (at central every C, Go and Python Server handle runs through the persistence wrapper, which is outside the theorem boundary by owner decision; the Beacon functions of the bindings map onto the experiment's commands by the ledger's reviewed classification (RT), not by a proof, so the headlines, which each concern a whole deployment of one Server at index 0 and its Beacons, reach Beacons used through the bindings only in a deployment whose Server is the plain Rust `Server`, and no deployment that uses only the bindings is covered); any later-quantum origin, UKS, binding or authentication statement (the later mode stays confidentiality only, because CE-12 has none); the closure of `ctGapTerm` at the production instance (the D-ct lane, which closes it in the experiment's forms by its `_dct` closed forms, reached central at `16956129` after this build and is merged here before the integration; the production forms, like the F-ct2 forms they restate, keep the term explicit, and the CE guide's D-ct notes give the status of the owner flag on it); a designation-free form of the pin or plaintext fields, the root, and plaintext-linked UKS (as in CE-12 P4); running time (bills count coins and oracle calls); and any statement about other identity indices than 0, a varying `checks`, or the Rust code beyond the reviewed transcription. (Follow-up: the production forms of the D-ct closed forms, whose bounds have `epsCt` in place of `ctGapTerm`, are in `PqxdhDeploymentProductionHeadlinesDct`; the 129 forms above are unchanged and keep the term explicit where they carry it, see the section "D-ct follow-up: production forms of the closed ciphertext-gap headlines".)
