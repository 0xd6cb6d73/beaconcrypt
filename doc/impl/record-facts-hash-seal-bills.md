# Total hidden sealing attempts

`PqxdhRecordFactsHashSealBills.program_seals` proves that the complete adaptive record-layer `PqxdhRecordLiftProtocol.program` makes at most `budget.sends` hidden sealing service requests. The theorem applies to every permitted mode, arbitrary environment, initial/source codec and routing functions, and every adversary satisfying the existing outer command budget. It keeps every typed reply continuation. There is no selected target, honest-candidate condition, successful-writer requirement or extra environment framing premise.

`sealCost` counts one at every `.sealing` request and zero at the other service constructors. The count is therefore taken before any sealing rejection or writer failure. `sealCost_category` identifies it with the existing `.seal` category ledger. `world_seal_cost` shows that the corresponding World bill is precisely the record-send indicator. `handler_seals` transports that one-send allowance through the actual server or beacon handler, including inactive owners, absent peers, caller-known and hidden kernels, empty input, exhausted chains and writer failure. `program_seals` combines this handler fact with the existing outer `program_record_bound` and the stateful weighted simulation theorem.

The absence of an additional `Frame` premise is deliberate: the existing record-send bound follows from `budget.Respects`, while the environment interface exposes effects and capabilities rather than arbitrary record commands. Effect-query allowances remain necessary for their separate ledgers. This theorem gives the total source sealing-attempt cap needed by the combined private/shared digest compiler; it does not yet prove that compiler's complete digest-query count or a collision probability. Registration remains in its separate lane. Native primitive calls, ordinary hash work, lookup samples and raw verification attempts retain their existing separate accounting. No primitive or security assumption is added.
