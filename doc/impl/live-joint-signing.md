# Live Phase-1 signing through the joint identity service

`PqxdhLiveJointSigning` connects the executable live bundle from HB-118 to the actual dynamically created joint Ed25519/X25519 identity service. Its eleven checked statements preserve the entire prior private seed pool, complete signing/service history, local generator/writer state, native phase and optional serialized reply. The executable bundle receives only its created public handle. The seed appears only in the mathematical source interpreter used to state equality.

`joint_complete` shows that arbitrary joint-service programs preserve the whole local auxiliary state. `serialization_complete` shows that arbitrary writer programs preserve the whole joint-service state. These are equalities of complete evaluated programs, not marginal output distributions. `generation_complete` similarly keeps the joint state across the local one-time generator. They permit the protocol continuation to inspect all modeled source state without discarding correlations.

`signing_complete` consumes the actual seed lookup at the live handle and runs the three source signatures in prekey, one-time, PQ order. It appends them to every prior observation. This lookup is a functional ownership invariant supplied at creation and preserved by the final two statements; it is not an assumption that a network-selected candidate is honest, authenticated or unchanged. No claim that the entire history has only three entries is made. `writing_complete` retains the exact optional serialization and publication outcome. In particular a failed writer keeps the three already-issued signatures and the original native publication behavior.

`afterGenerated_source` combines native preparation, actual signatures and the writer. `bundle_source` also includes the original generator position: Fresh draws the one-time pair before preparation, FreshWithCoins uses the retained pair, and other phases reject without a new draw. All intermediate private state and all final replies remain in the equality. These statements use the existing production refinement of the native bundle and the ordinary functional primitive contracts. They prove no primitive implementation property, secrecy bound or forgery bound.

`sourceAfter_uniform` bills at most qWriter actual random queries for the writer on every candidate and auxiliary state. `bundle_uniform` bills at most qGenerate + qWriter random queries for the complete created-handle bundle. The callback bounds include their full random-query work; no ambient oracle is made free. The existing bundle service ledger separately bills at most one local generation, three joint signing requests and one writer invocation. The random-query statement does not assign zero running time to deterministic signing, preparation, transcript copying, serialization or primitive evaluation. Those retain their explicit functional and running-time contracts.

`sourceAfter_seeds` and `bundle_seeds` prove that every supported outcome preserves the entire created seed list, even a failed writer. Thus later calls retain the original handle lookup rather than assuming a new private state. Dynamic native owner allocation, byte-based peer lookup, full global target selection, selected KEM embedding and source compromise routing remain consuming work. The published interface does not turn this local invariant into authentication or silently expose a seed to a reduction. Snapshots, persistence and restoration are outside the experiment.
