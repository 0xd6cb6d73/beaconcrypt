# Retained cross-handle commitment conflict

`PqxdhRecordFactsBindingLedger` defines the internal source evidence needed for the final transcript. It requires the exact accepted-receive and successful-publication witnesses, plus the actual shared-ROM commitment witness whenever the corresponding final handle is caller-known. These four fields are ordinary operational invariants to be derived from the full source ledger execution. They are not primitive contracts or assumptions of a completed protocol security theorem. In particular, the source induction must justify persistence from the time of each entry to the final control and private state; one-way hidden-slot preservation alone does not justify inferring historical hidden ownership from a final hidden handle.

`PqxdhRecordFactsBindingEvent` proves six guarded statements. `records_conflict` analyzes the four caller-known/hidden combinations for two successful records with the same complete decoded frame. Both caller-known handles contradict the event's requirement that at least one final handle has a hidden slot. A hidden record paired with a caller-known record gives an equality between a hidden seal commitment and an answer in the actual shared hash cache. Two hidden records give equal commitments at distinct material labels: if their labels were equal, the receiving slot and opposite physical direction would establish precisely the counterpart relationship excluded by the event. The proof retains the actual raw bytes, returned ciphertext, accepted plaintext witness, roles and installed slot; it assumes neither endpoint was selected honestly.

`cross_accept_conflict` applies that case analysis to the unchanged `CrossAccept` definition. It extracts the literal receive entry and successful publication entry from the complete final transcript and consumes all four fields of `LedgerEvidence`. Publication may occur before or after the receive; the theorem imposes no additional ordering restriction. The result, `DigestConflict`, refers only to successful seal histories and the original shared-ROM cache. It deliberately does not assert a private suffix-cache witness in the counted source world.

`conflict_retained` and `conflict_observe` prove that this conflict depends only on data retained by the checked counter/private-cache observation. `run_conflict_observation` then applies the existing full source `run_observation` theorem to obtain equality of Boolean conflict-event distributions between the actual CTX run and the erased run, for every adaptive continuation and with every hidden coordinate in replay or random mode. The mapping retains the exact client computation and uses only the retained histories and shared cache to decide the event. It does not equate costs or remove original sampler charges from the source bill.

`conflict_collision` uses the separately proved erased-world `AllCached` invariant to turn a retained conflict into the ordinary combined-cache collision event. Hidden/hidden conflicts use private inputs `(label, outerSuffix nonce context tag)` with different labels; hidden/caller-known conflicts use one private input and the exact ordinary hash bytes. Thus the inputs are provably distinct, and at least one has the private input constructor. The classifier is fixed by that constructor before sampling. No injectivity, collision resistance or chosen-output property is introduced as an assumption.

This stage adds no oracle calls, reduction loss, free ambient work or new query bound. It supplies the event inclusion and distribution transport needed to apply the independently checked lazy-oracle bound after the full source ledger invariant and compiler request caps are established. The complete source-ledger discharge, combined-cache compiler and global query accounting remain necessary before the frozen binding endpoint can be claimed. No primitive implementation, persistence or snapshot property is involved.
