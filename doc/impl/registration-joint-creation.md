# Native registration creation through indexed services

`PqxdhRegistrationJointCreation` connects the published `PqxdhRegistrationSchedule.create` operation to the existing joint Ed25519/X25519 identity and ML-KEM services. Both sides use the same source draw: a fresh 32-byte identity seed, a fresh 32-byte prekey scalar, and the actual KEM key generator, in that order. The source's later one-time generation stays in the bundle operation. This is the typed, post-admission constructor body; the raw constructor's initialization and 32-byte pin-parsing boundary remains in `RegistrationScheduleSurface`.

The executable `client` receives an identity creation index and its correlated public identity, the actual private prekey, and a KEM creation index and public key. It receives no identity seed or KEM secret. Those values remain in the existing private interpreters. The service indices and the source owner address are different fields. This module does not identify either by public-key equality.

`client_source` proves the complete evaluated client equation, including both private service states and their entire prior histories. `native_source` proves the corresponding literal scheduler equation, including every earlier owner, the complete shared Server and pending state, both primitive caches and the newly appended native owner. `client_material` expresses both sides through the same three-value sampler. These equations do not exchange generators or drop private fields.

`native_continuation` and `client_continuation` retain those equations under arbitrary continuations of the complete respective results. Thus the proof supplies more than equality of public-key marginals. The sampler's `Material` record and the private service projection are mathematical observations used to prove these equations; neither is an extra adversary reply or an input to the executable client.

`client_present` proves that the ordinary lazy identity generator cannot produce the client's exhausted-service rejection branch. `service_owns` recovers the exact generated seed and KEM pair at their actual newly appended indices. It works even when their public encodings coincide with earlier ones. Earlier private prefixes and observations remain present in the full distribution equations.

`client_bills` counts one joint creation request, one KEM creation request and 32 local byte-valued uniform queries on every reply path. `client_uniform` also interprets the private services: the complete uniform bill is `64 + qKeygen`, where `qKeygen` bounds every uniform query of the evaluated KEM/base callback. The existing `ServerAuthSourceCosts.keygen_uniform` supplies `qKeygen = keygenUniform + baseUniform * keygenBase` from the unchanged resource contract. Neither base callbacks nor the identity seed draw are free. The result is query accounting, not a runtime bound.

The nine statements introduce no cryptographic assumption or primitive implementation proof. They preserve the existing functional setup, scalar/seed samplers and KEM/base interface. There is no security advantage, chosen target, honest-selection condition, authentication premise or public-key injectivity hypothesis in these statements. Dynamic bundle/finish routing, owner-to-service invariants through arbitrary schedules, actual reveals and reporting, selection losses and the global cryptographic/symbolic composition remain separate work. This constructor correspondence alone does not remove the older root theorem's zero-create/zero-sign caller conditions.
