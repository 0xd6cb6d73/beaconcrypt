There is no new argument; the name `PqxdhRecordLift` is the final module name of the brief, which the world track left free.

## `PqxdhRecordLiftSwap` (10 guarded theorems)

A generic coupling of uniform-coin computations. `Swap R x y` holds when the two computations are built node by node from: related final values; the same coin on both sides with the right coin relabelled by a bijection of its range (`query_bind`); or an escape (`product`), two unrelated first parts whose reachable outputs are pairwise related, followed by coupled continuations. `Swap.evalDist_map` is the distributional conclusion: Boolean observations that agree on related final values have equal laws, because a bijection preserves the uniform law of a coin and an escape pairs every output with every output. `Swap.mono`, `bind`, `map`, `refl`, `refl_support`, `escape` and `query` compose couplings, `support_keep` transports a state invariant through a simulation, and `Swap.simulateQ_run` transports single-query couplings through the simulation of one program under two stateful implementations: from related states every query is coupled with equal replies and related states or with both states escaped (`SimRel`), and the escape predicates are kept by every later query. Unlike the hiding coupling `PqxdhRecordLiftHiding.Coupled`, this one relabels coins and lets escaped runs diverge, which the left-or-right and fresh-message arguments need.

## `PqxdhRecordLiftFlat` (35 guarded theorems)

The flattened CTX world. `ctxRun_flat` writes every CTX-mode world `ctxRun modes replace` as its startup (`startup`), the installation of the constructed Server (`install`) and then one simulation of the observed adversary under `fullImpl`, the secrecy ledger over `nativeImpl`; `nativeImpl` is the native dispatch over the compiled client over the CTX interpreter, flattened onto the joint state of environment state, control state and private CTX state by `QueryImpl.mapStateTBase` and `flattenStateT` (`NativeState`). `fullImpl_run` and `nativeImpl_run` unfold one query. Facts on this world: a native coin is one uniform query whose state update (`coinStep`) does not depend on the reply (`nativeImpl_coin`); with the zeroing replacement `zeros` of hidden seal plaintexts (any key modes), a native send of either of two equal-length plaintexts on a handle without a caller-known kernel (`KnownAt`) is the same computation (`nativeImpl_send_zeros`, from the send-admission lemmas `beginSend_context`, `normalBegin_context`, `startContext_normalBegin`, the sealing lemmas `selectedInput_congr`, `hiddenSealing_zeros`, `ctxInterpreter_sealing_zeros`, and the program lemmas `sendProgram_zeros`, `ownerSend_zeros`, `serverStep_send_zeros`, `beaconStep_send_zeros`, `native_serverSend_run`, `native_beaconSend_run`; absent handles make no query); a caller-known kernel stays caller-known under every native command (`nativeImpl_known`, from `ownerRun_known`, `handler_peerKnown`, `handler_ownerKnown`, `handler_known`; Server installation needs a vacant peer key ID, Beacon installation an owner in `initSent`, and no capability removes a peer or an established owner); every ledger step appends one transcript entry (`ledger_append`) and the test session's handle is the first admissible test's (`LedgerInv`, `ledger_inv`). `support_run_fst` and `support_keep_oracle` are generic support facts.

## `PqxdhRecordLiftLor` (24 guarded theorems)

`idealRecords_lor` proves `LorStatement setup codec route serverKid bounds n` as defined: in mode P, for every environment, adversary, public decision and public eligibility on `PublicHiding` (no `Frame` or budget premise), `Pr[lorGuess] = 1/2 · Pr[lorEligible]` in `idealRecords`. Both events depend on a view only through its hiding projection (`lorGuess_hiding`, `lorEligible_hiding`, from `testsHidden_hiding`, `testBit_hidingView` and `PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.admittedTest_view`), so by `PqxdhRecordLiftHiding.hiding_replace` their laws equal those in the random-ciphertext world with zeroed hidden plaintexts (`law_zeros`, stated for every observation that factors through the hiding projection). That world is coupled with itself (`world_couple`) with the native coin of the first admitted test relabelled by `Fin.rev`, so the bit is negated (`fullImpl_test_first`, `decide_rev`): queries that send no test message commute with negating the ledger's bit (`ledger_flip_forward`, `ledger_flip_fresh`, `ledger_flip_refused`, `commute_couple`); a test on a handle without a caller-known kernel sends the other message by the same computation (`send_couple`, `fullImpl_test_session`); a test on a caller-known handle is an escape (`Escaped`), kept by every later query (`escaped_keep`), and an escaped run has no hidden-test win (`lorGuess_escaped`). On related final states the decision's win equals the negated decision's win on the other side (`lorGuess_flip`), so the two wins have the same law; they exclude each other and together are eligibility with all tests hidden (`lorEligible_split`, `evalDist_or`), hence each is half (`lor_zeros`).

## `PqxdhRecordLiftFresh` (40 guarded theorems)

`idealRecords_fresh` proves `FreshGuessStatement setup codec route serverKid bounds n` as defined: in mode P, for every environment, adversary and claim read from the adversary's output (no `Frame` or budget premise), `Pr[freshEvent] ≤ 2^-min` in `idealRecords`, where `min` is `bounds.min`. The event depends on a view only through its hiding projection (`freshHit_hiding`, `freshEvent_hiding`), so `PqxdhRecordLiftLor.law_zeros` moves it to the zeroed world. There, for a claimed index `i`, a message length `ℓ` and an offset vector `d` of `ℓ` bytes, `world_couple` couples the world with itself with the native coin of byte `j` of the `i`-th admitted fresh message relabelled by adding `d_j` modulo 256 (`bytes_couple`, `shiftByte_coin`, `fullImpl_fresh`, `target_couple`), so that message is shifted by `d` (`shiftFrom`, `extend`, `shiftLedger`) and nothing else changes: other queries commute with the shift (`ledger_shift_forward`, `ledger_shift_test`, `ledger_shift_fresh`, `shiftLedger_append`, `commute_couple`), the send of the message or of its shift is the same computation on a handle without a caller-known kernel (`send_couple`), a send on a caller-known handle is an escape (`FreshEscaped`, kept by `freshEscaped_keep` since fresh entries are only appended, `ledger_fresh_prefix`), and in an escaped run the message's handle holds no slot (`handleSlot_known`), so neither side hits (`hitAt_escaped`, `shiftedHit_escaped`). The relation also carries that every fresh message has at least `min` bytes (`FreshLong`, `ledger_freshLong`, `uniformBytes_length`). Hence the hit with claim `(i, ℓ)` has the probability of the shifted hit for every `d` (`hitAt_shift`); the `256^ℓ` shifted hits exclude each other (`shiftedHit_disjoint`, `extend_offsets`), each implies the claim (`shiftedHit_key`), and none is possible for `ℓ < min`, so `Pr[hit at (i, ℓ)] ≤ 256^-min · Pr[claim is (i, ℓ)]` (`hitAt_bound`). Every hit is a hit at its own claim (`freshEvent_cover`) and claims exclude each other (`keyAt_unique`), so summing over `(i, ℓ)` gives at most `256^-min ≤ 2^-min` (`fresh_zeros`). This is the weighting of the remaining-work plan of the world track: bounding each index by `2^-min` separately would only give the number of fresh messages times `2^-min`.

## Remaining work

`OriginStatement` and `BindingStatement` are open. Both were checked by reading against the definitions and the Rust-derived source model and neither was found false. They are stated for both observation modes, and the publication and binding parts are statistical bounds over the whole adaptive run, so they need more infrastructure than the left-or-right and fresh-message facts. The flattened world of `PqxdhRecordLiftFlat` and the couplings of `PqxdhRecordLiftSwap` are the intended base. The plan, in order:

1. Structural conjuncts of `OriginStatement` (`unproducedEvent` has probability zero in `idealAuth` and `idealRecords`). A support invariant of `fullImpl` (any key modes, any replacement) at query boundaries, where the private writer buffer `pending` is empty: (a) every successful seal in the replay dictionary `successfulSeals` of the CTX local state `keys L` of a coordinate `L` has its frame `⟨sequence, sender, record.encode⟩` in the produced log `base.produced`, tagged with the handle that holds the hidden kernel that seals at `L` (the sealing service logs the frame before the writer result, `hiddenSealing`, and `publish tag` moves `pending` into `produced` with the command's tag); (b) a hidden kernel of slot `s` in role `R` seals only at coordinates of slot `s` in the sending direction of `R` and opens only at coordinates of slot `s` in the sending direction of the opposite role (`PqxdhRecordLiftWorldProvenance.labeledKernel_oriented`, `owner_receive_oriented` and the coordinate facts of `PqxdhRecordLiftProtocolHistory`); (c) installed hidden kernels are never removed or replaced and each slot is used at most once per role (`Unused`, `markUsed`, the persistence argument of `nativeImpl_known` for hidden kernels); (d) an accepted hidden opening is a replay match (`AdaptiveCtxReplayDictionary.replayPlaintext`: same nonce, associated data and encoded record), and a match equates the received frame with the sealed one (the record context `Pqxdh.RecordAD` that `replayPlaintext` compares carries the frame's sequence and sender (`seq`, `sid`) in addition to the 153 associated-data bytes, matching `LE64(seq) || LE64(sender_kid)` in the Rust commitment transcript of `beaconcrypt-core/src/commitment.rs`; the 153 bytes themselves (`build_associated_data` in `beaconcrypt-core/src/pqxdh.rs`) hold only the tagged Server and Beacon identity keys, `PQXDH_INFO` and `SYM_RATCHET_INFO`; the seal side builds the context in `RawCtxSelectedMessageSealing.selectedInput` and the open side in `RawCtxKeyFreeDecoder.target`, so equal contexts give equal sequence and sender, and `RawCtxFrameDecoding.fromPayload_record` with `CtxRomAuth.decodeRecord_romRecord` gives equal payloads; no codec contract). The transcript side uses `ledger_append`: an entry `received = some (receiver, raw, .Some decrypted)` is a raw-receive query whose reply came from an accepted opening of the kernel on `receivingHandle route receiver raw` (`serverStep` routes with the same `route`).
2. Publication conjuncts of `OriginStatement` (`unpublishedEvent ≤ publicationTerm budget = 2 · R · S / 2^512` in `idealAuth`, `idealRecords` and `randomRecords`). On top of item 1: a seal whose writer succeeded is published in the same query (its ciphertext is the send's reply, recorded by the ledger; `Entry.published` covers sends, tests and fresh messages), so an accepted frame not published before is that of a seal whose writer failed. Then an identical-until-bad comparison with the world in which replay matches against failed-writer seals are disabled (a new generic lemma on `Swap`-style couplings: `Pr[F in x] ≤ Pr[G in y] + Pr[bad]` when the runs agree until a bad flag that both sides raise together), and in that world a two-guess bound per attempt and seal: the seal's private 64-byte commitment (one `AdaptiveCtxSharedReplay.privateLookup` draw, never written to the shared cache) affects the run only through the writer's success bit, so the attempt's commitment equals it with probability at most `2 · 2^-512` (the attempt is one of two guesses, one per value of the bit, each independent of the commitment; the relabelling technique of `PqxdhRecordLiftFresh` applies to the commitment's coins). A union bound over at most `R` raw attempts and `S` seals (`Budget.Respects`, the counts of `PqxdhRecordLiftWorldBudget`) gives the term.
3. `BindingStatement` (`crossEvent ≤ bindingTerm` in `idealAuth` and `idealRecords`, premise `FrameReadBack`). On top of item 1: with `FrameReadBack` a ciphertext returned on a handle reads back as that handle's produced frame, so a cross acceptance on non-partner handles needs two different seals with equal records, which forces a private commitment to equal a shared-cache output (at most `R + S + H` of them, `PqxdhRecordLiftWorldBudget.run_hash`) or another private commitment. A generic collision lemma (each fresh uniform 64-byte draw equals one of at most `M` values fixed before it with probability at most `M / 2^512`), summed over the draws with the budget counts, bounds that event by `bindingTerm`; on its complement `CrossAccept` is impossible by item 1. `DecoderAgreement` is a premise of `OriginStatement` only; the plan does not expect to need it.
