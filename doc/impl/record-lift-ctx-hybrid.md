# Record-lift CTX integrity half: hybrids, link, availability, selected-key law, telescope and bills (items 4 and 5, stage S4)

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxHybrid`, `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxLink`, `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxAvailability`, `PqxdhRecordLiftKeyCut`, `PqxdhRecordLiftCtx` and `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxBills` are the six modules of the CTX track's integrity half (stage S4 of the takeover plan `/tmp/bc-arc-scratch/items45-takeover/takeover-plan.md`, step X1). The first proves that the all-real CTX world of `PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal` is exactly the chain-ideal world `idealChains` and defines the key-indexed hybrid family that the integrity half of `ctx_lift` (`PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.CtxAuthLiftStatement`) walks through. The second reduces one step of that family to the ordinary one-key CTX challenger of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModes`, with a key-free reduction client. The third proves that every keyed request of every CTX world is at a coordinate already present in the chain table and within the hybrid index range, and identifies the last hybrid with `idealAuth`. The fourth proves the selected-key law, the fifth telescopes the hybrids into the integrity target `CtxAuthLiftTarget` for the explicit reduction family `ctxReduction` from the premise `StepBilled bills`, and the sixth proves that premise for the explicit bills `ctxBills`. `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxBills.ctxAuthLiftTarget` is the unconditional integrity target (both forms, zero refusal term), and `ctxAuthLiftStatement` gives `CtxAuthLiftStatement` for every refusal term. Their 29, 27, 80, 39, 6 and 34 guarded theorems use no assumption and no contract.

## Source equality

The all-real CTX world is `ctxRun (fun _ => .real) id`: every hidden opening and sealing becomes one keyed request at its material coordinate and is answered by that coordinate's real one-key CTX state (`AdaptiveCtxSharedModes.localImpl true`) with the key and nonce of the coordinate's table entry and the world's shared BLAKE2b cache; every other service is `idealChains`' service. `allReal_bare` proves that this world and `idealChains` have the same law of the complete client result together with the counter-erased private state `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocol.Bare` (shared cache, effect ledger, seeds, full chain table, tagged produced frames, pending writer log); only the per-coordinate CTX states and the native and uniform analysis counters are erased. `allReal_view` and `allReal_decision` restate this for the permitted view `PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.View` and for every cache-free decision on it, which is the form the CTX targets use.

The proof goes service by service. A hidden opening (`opening_bare`) first applies the source payload parser and the actual total getters, as the native opening does; on a parsed, present and admitted opening the keyed raw request carries the universal envelope (`RawCtxUniversalDecoder.pack` of `RawCtxUniversalDecoder.envelope`), and `real_open_cache` shows, through the existing `RawCtxSelectedOpenConsumer.call_cache`, that the real one-key opening with the coordinate's key and nonce returns exactly the native shared-ROM opening's optional plaintext and cache. A hidden seal (`sealing_bare`) with a nonce that its coordinate has not attempted returns exactly the native seal (`real_seal_cache`): the same record, cache update, logged frame (also when the writer fails) and API reply; an associated datum of the wrong width is rejected identically on both sides. Every other service is the chain world's service on the base state (`base_bare`, from `ctxInterpreter_base` and the existing `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocol.bare_query`).

The real one-key CTX state refuses a repeated nonce, while the native seal never refuses; the proof shows that the refusal never fires. `Unsealed state past` says that every coordinate outside the attempted-seal history `past` has attempted no nonce. It holds initially (`initial_unsealed`) and every supported service extends it by exactly its own sealing coordinate (`step_unsealed`, using `opening_nonces`, `sealing_other` and `base_keys`). The existing syntactic fact `PqxdhRecordLiftProtocolHistory.program_fresh` says that on every reply path of the frozen program a hidden sealing names a coordinate outside the attempted history, and `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocol.program_admitted` gives the opening admission facts. `schedule_bare` is the induction over the whole adaptive program that combines these; `allReal_bare` instantiates it at the initial state and composes it with `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocol.bare_schedule` for `idealChains`.

## Hybrid family

`hybridModes positions cut` puts a coordinate in replay mode exactly when `PqxdhRecordLiftChainOrder.selected positions cut` selects it, that is when it is bounded (its depth is below `positions`) and its depth-major index is below `cut`; every other coordinate is real. `hybrid positions cut` is the CTX world under these modes. The first hybrid is the all-real world (`hybridModes_zero`) and has the view law of `idealChains` (`hybrid_zero_view`). After all `positions * (n * 2)` steps exactly the bounded coordinates are in replay mode (`hybridModes_last`). One step changes the mode of its own coordinate only (`hybridModes_unchanged`), from real to replay (`hybridModes_step`). With `positions = Budget.sends + 51 * Budget.receives` the steps are indexed by `Fin (PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.hops n budget)`, the index set of the targets' sums.

## One hybrid step as the one-key challenger

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxLink` generalizes the CTX world to an explicit key function: `ctxRunWith keyOf` reads the key of each hidden coordinate as `keyOf` of the chain table, and `ctxRunWith_tableKey` shows that the table key gives `ctxRun` back. `selectedKey target key` supplies the selected coordinate's key from outside and keeps every other coordinate's table key; `selectedModes target real modes` puts the selected coordinate in real (`true`) or replay (`false`) mode and keeps every other mode. Every explicit-key world erases, service by service and for every state, to a counter-erased CTX interpreter (`ctxInterpreterWith_erase`, `ctxInterpreterWith_schedule`): only the native and uniform analysis counters disappear.

The reduction `reduction target modes` is the key-free private interpreter of one step. It keeps the counter-erased chain-world state (seeds, the full chain table that it samples itself, effects, produced and pending frames). Public coins and public BLAKE2b queries are forwarded to the challenger through a reserved known-call slot (`coinForward`, `hashForward`), so the challenger's shared cache is the world's cache. The selected coordinate's hidden openings and seals become the challenger's own requests (`routeKeyed`). Every other hidden coordinate's openings and seals become known calls (`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModes.KnownCall`) at an injective slot (`slotOf`, slot 0 being reserved), with that coordinate's table key and in real mode exactly when its mode is real; this is where the hybrid needs the replay-capable known calls of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModes`, which the existing single-cut reduction of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocol` (all other keys real) does not provide. Caller-known native records run with their commitment hashes forwarded (`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocol.knownRom`). The reduction never receives a key of the selected coordinate; it does sample the selected coordinate's table entry, whose key bytes it never reads.

`linked_step` shows, for every service and every state and without any reachability premise, that the reduction run against the challenger's source state realizes the counter-erased CTX world with the selected key supplied externally: the selected coordinate's CTX state is the challenger's, every other coordinate's is its known slot's (`keysOf`, `keysOf_selected`, `keysOf_other`, `keysOf_reserved`), and the challenger's cache is the world's. `linked_schedule` extends this to every program, and `link` concludes: the challenger's real or replay game `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModes.sourceGame` against the key-free client `linkClient` (the whole program under the reduction, then the decision on the permitted view with its coins forwarded) is the CTX world in which the selected coordinate's key is a fresh uniform key and the selected coordinate is in the challenger's mode. For step `s` of the hybrid family with coordinate `entryAt s`, the real game has the modes of the hybrid before the step and the replay game the modes of the hybrid after it (`selectedModes_hybrid_real`, `selectedModes_hybrid_replay`); the hybrids never use random mode (`hybridModes_notRandom`), so every other coordinate fits a known call.

## Availability of every keyed request

`PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.materialAt` reads a coordinate's key and nonce from the chain table with a total all-zero fallback, and the hybrid index of `PqxdhRecordLiftChainOrder` covers only coordinates of depth below `positions budget`. `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxAvailability` shows that no CTX world ever reaches either case, for every key function, mode assignment and adversary within its budget.

The client side is a property of the compiled program alone. `historyTable past` is the table whose present coordinates are exactly the lookup history `past`. `ServiceSafe client past` says that on every reply path every private lookup is `Available` against the history before it and every hidden opening or seal reads the material of a coordinate already looked up (`Avail`). `program_history` proves `ServiceSafe program []` for the compiled protocol program: hidden receives and sends are handled by the existing reference lemmas of `PqxdhRecordLiftChainCompiler` (`resume_references`, `finish_references`, `successor_available`) on the history table (`receive_history`, `send_history`), the admission facts of `PqxdhRecordLiftProtocolHistory` hold for every reply, and the stored kernels of the Server peers and Beacon owners stay ready against the growing history (`ControlReady`, `ownerRun_history`, `serverStep_history`, `beaconStep_history`, `handler_history`, `schedule_history`). `program_lookups` bills the lookups: at most one per send and 51 per raw receive (`kernelSend_lookups`, `kernelReceive_lookups`, from `PqxdhRecordLiftOpaqueBounds`), hence at most `positions budget` on every path (`world_lookups`, from `PqxdhRecordLiftBudget.program_record_bound`). Since every new lookup is `Available`, the history table's depth stays below the history's length (`history_depth`), so every looked-up coordinate is bounded by `positions budget` (`history_bounded`).

The transfer to private interpreters is generic. `certified_map` takes any private interpreter, on any state type, whose chain table grows and holds every looked-up coordinate (with an optional state invariant), and another interpreter that simulates it through a state map on every request whose opened or sealed coordinates (`Touches`) are present and bounded; along a certified program the whole runs are related by the same map. `program_map` instantiates the certificate for the compiled program. For the CTX world with an arbitrary keyed implementation (`ctxInterpreterOf`, of which `ctxInterpreterWith` is the explicit-key instance, `ctxInterpreterWith_of`), every service grows the table (`chain_step`) and openings and seals depend on their keyed implementation only at their material coordinate (`opening_congr`, `sealing_congr`); so keyed implementations that agree on present coordinates within `positions budget` give the same world (`program_congr`), and so do key functions and modes that agree there (`ctxRunWith_congr`). With `hybridModes_last`, `hybrid_last` proves that the last hybrid is `idealAuth`.

## The selected-key law

`PqxdhRecordLiftKeyCut.key_cut_view` proves, for every mode assignment, coordinate `target` and adversary within its budget, that the view of `ctxRun modes` has the law of first sampling a uniform key and then running `ctxRunWith (selectedKey target key) modes`; `key_cut` and `key_cut_measure` restate it for every decision on the view, in the form of `link`. Replay mode also seals with the key (real ciphertexts), so the law is used on both sides of every hop.

The proof has three steps. `world keyed store target` is the CTX world whose lookup of `target`, on a miss, stores `store output` instead of the sampled output (`world_id`: storing the output itself is the CTX world). First, `table_eager` applies the existing one-shot eager-sampling lemma `PqxdhServerAuthEntryFactor.eager_run`: the CTX world has the law of sampling a 32-byte key up front and running the world whose target miss stores the sampled output with its key bytes replaced by the key (`keyStore`). At the target's miss the two samplings agree under every continuation (`miss_eager`, from `PqxdhRecordLiftProjectionCut.split_continuation`); every other request does not use the key, and once the target is present nothing does. Both worlds read every key from the table, so this step needs no availability premise. Second, `selected_table`: in the key-storing world, reading the target's key from the table (`tableKey`) or using the supplied key directly (`selectedKey`) gives the same world, by `program_map` with the invariant `KeyStored` (a present target entry carries the supplied key, `world_preserve`, `chain_frame`) and the availability certificate (every keyed request is at a present coordinate). Third, `stored_view`: a selected-key world never reads the key bytes of the target's entry, because the chain read (`chain_mask`), every nonce (`nonce_mask`), every other key (`selectedKey_mask`) and the hidden opening and sealing programs (`hiddenOpening_mask`, `hiddenSealing_mask`) ignore them. Masking them (`maskState`) is therefore a simulation of every service (`world_mask`, with `base_mask` for the services that do not read the table), and the stored key bytes affect neither the client result nor the produced frames.

## The telescope

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtx.ctxReduction` is the explicit CTX reduction family: hop `step`'s one-key adversary is `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModes.decisionClient` of `stepClient`, the link client of `entryAt step` under the modes before the step. `game_real` and `game_replay` identify the hybrids before and after the step with the challenger's real and replay games (selected-key law, `link`, `selectedModes_hybrid_real`, `selectedModes_hybrid_replay`), so `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModeBounds.selected_integrity` bounds each hop by its `integrityLoss` at the step client's hash and raw caps (`hop_bound`). `ctxAuthLiftBool_of_billed` sums the hops with `PqxdhComposition.finiteHybridBound_of_hop_bounds` (coefficient one; the factor 2 on INT-CTXT is inside `integrityLoss`), identifies the end points with `idealChains` (`allReal_decision`) and `idealAuth` (`hybrid_last`), and derives the second conjunct `CtxBounded` from `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModeBounds.decision_budgets`. `ctxAuthLiftTarget_of_billed` gives `CtxAuthLiftTarget` (both forms, through `ctxAuthLiftStatement_of_bool`). The premise `StepBilled bills` says that every step client stays within the bills' uniform (a private 64-byte replay cache fill counting 64), shared-hash, seal (below `2^96`) and raw caps.

## The bills

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxBills` proves `StepBilled ctxBills` (`stepBilled`). Program level: in each ledger of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocolBounds.categoryCost` (uniform: 64 per seed allocation, 76 per lookup, one per coin; hash: one per public hash, hidden opening or seal, and caller-known send or receive; raw: one per hidden opening), every World request stays within `worldBill` (`handler_categories`, through `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProgramBounds.kernel_send` and `kernel_receive`), the World program within `programBill` (`world_categories`, from `PqxdhRecordLiftBudget.program_bound` for coins, hashes and administrative capabilities, the only effects that can allocate a seed, and `program_record_bound` for sends and raw receives), and the compiled program therefore too (`program_categories`). The target coordinate is sealed at most once on every path (`fresh_target_seal`, `program_target_seal`, from `PqxdhRecordLiftProtocolHistory.program_fresh`), which gives the seal cap one, below `2^96` (`Budget` itself has no cap below `2^96` on sends). Step level: per service, the step reduction forwards each coin and public hash as one request (`coinForward_route`, `hashForward_route`), draws at most 64 coins per allocation (`allocate_total`) and 76 per lookup (`bareLookup_total`), routes at most one request per hidden opening or seal (`opening_route`, `sealing_route`) and makes at most one hash per caller-known send or receive (`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocolBounds.knownRom_total`). `reduction_step` packages this per category, and its instances give the five categories of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModeBounds` (`reduction_uniform`, `reduction_hash`, `reduction_private`, `reduction_seal`, `reduction_raw`); `run_bound` lifts a service-level weighted bound through the whole program and `link_route` adds the decision's own coins. `ctxBills` charges the program's uniform bill plus the decision's coins plus 64 coins per possible private replay-cache fill (counted by the hash bill), the hash bill (equal to `PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.sharedHashTotal`, `hash_bill`), one seal and `Budget.receives` raw requests.
