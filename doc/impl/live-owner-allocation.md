# Native owner allocation and joint identity ownership

`PqxdhLiveOwnerCreation` connects the actual live constructor to a private growable owner registry. Its nine statements establish source ownership at this allocation boundary. The caller receives only the new native owner index and correlated public identity. The complete private role retains the generated prekey and KEM pair, pinned server binding, source Fresh phase and instrumentation. The identity seed remains in the joint service. The native owner index is the prior registry length; the identity-service index is the prior joint seed-list length. They are not assumed equal, and different generated public bytes are not assumed unequal.

`empty_valid` starts ownership without an initial representation assumption. `new_represents` proves that the fresh role's internal identity handle resolves to its actual newly generated seed and that seed's actual public identity. `prior_represents` proves that joint creation preserves the lookup and public identity of every prior represented owner. `install_valid` combines these facts for the whole registry. This representation condition concerns honest source-owned objects, not an adversary-selected registration candidate or a network authentication event.

`create_source` is an equality of the complete evaluated allocation operation. It executes the source identity, prekey and KEM generators at their original position, installs their complete output, and retains every earlier owner and joint observation. It moves no draw across a later continuation. `create_valid` consumes support in that same actual execution and derives the representation invariant for every resulting owner. No source outcome equality or private-key availability is supplied as an external premise.

`allocated_lookup` identifies the public owner index and the full new role at that index. `allocated_prior` keeps every earlier role unchanged at its existing index. `create_uniform` bills at most 32 + qPrekey + qKeygen uniform queries, including the full interpreted ambient work of both local generators. Allocation and list operations do not add random draws; the theorem does not assign them zero running time or prove allocator availability.

The constructor retains its existing normal-return domain after the 32-byte pin parse and its functional primitive contracts. The registry does not add a public private-state reply. This module establishes the concrete allocation boundary; complete adaptive creation/service scheduling, representation preservation through all later handshake and record operations, selected-key replacement, compromise routing, target selection and the final symbolic event connection remain consuming work. No primitive implementation, persistence, snapshot or restoration property is proved.
