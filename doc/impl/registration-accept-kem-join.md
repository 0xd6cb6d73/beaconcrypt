# Raw-accept cut joined to the report compiler, the Phase-1 EUF bound and the selected ML-KEM challenge (stages A and B)

Status: stages A and B of the accept/KEM join plan. Stage A (four modules, 33 statements) joins the raw-accept cut to the initialized report compiler and to the ordinary joint-use weak-EUF family and classifies every reached cut. Stage B ports the reviewed source-suffix14 lane cohort (three modules, 14 statements, unchanged) and adds five modules with 49 statements: the KEM cut at the encapsulation of the selected raw accept, the raw-ordinal one-key ML-KEM IND-CCA hop with the ordinary ML-KEM correctness-error term instead of perfect correctness, and the query bills of the constructed KEM adversary. Every module passes a direct single-module Lean 4.34 check with an empty log and exact `#print axioms` guards that permit only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and the root module checks against the retained artifacts. Stage A was independently reviewed under a semantic and a hygiene lens, both approving with nits, and a follow-up commit addresses those findings. Stage B is not yet independently reviewed. Nothing is pushed. Both stages belong to handoff item 1 ("What has not been proved", item 1), which stops at the KEM-ideal game; the source-to-KEM bridge and the main theorem (stage C) and the opaque KEM-ideal continuation (stage D) are listed under "What stays open".

### What is proved

**Tag (M0).** `AcceptCutTag.tag work index` is `AcceptCut.scan work index >>= tagFinish` (`tag_split`, by definition). `tagFinish` resumes a pending cut exactly as `AcceptCut.finish` does, issuing the same raw accept and passing its reply to the original continuation, and additionally returns `some (raw, reply)`; a complete cut returns `none`. `finish_erase` and `tag_erase` show that forgetting the record gives `AcceptCut.finish` and the original program `work`, so the tag adds no request and changes no reply. `tag_bound` and `decision_bound` carry every `IsQueryBoundP` cap of `work` to `tag work index` and to `decisionClient decision work index`, the Boolean client `decision result && accepted tag` that later stages run. `accept_lt` shows that a reached pending cut has `index < cap` for any all-reply cap on raw accepts, and `accept_source` instantiates it at `qAccept = budget (.inl 3)`. Rejected earlier accepts are counted, so this bounds the raw-accept ordinal, not the encapsulation ordinal of `PqxdhRegistrationJointKemCut`; the two are different handles.

**Report split and naturality (M1).** `resume` restarts the reported command compiler from a recorded initialized outcome with the same Server, owner registry and native state, and `continuation` runs that resumption under the same identity reduction, from the reached local KEM/runtime state and source registry, and under the same report interpreter, from the reached monitor and reporting state. `commands_initialized_bind`, `initialized_bind` and `run_bind` show that the reported commands, the initialized report compiler and the report run of `program >>= next` are those of `program` followed by this resumption; the identity-reduction step is `TerminalReports.compiler_bind`. `cut_split` instantiates `run_bind` with `AcceptCut.scan_finish`: the report run of `work` is the report run of the raw-accept scan followed by `continuation … AcceptCut.finish`. `split` couples the two runs of the tagged program: it draws the reached outcome `r₁` of the scan and then the final outcome `r₂ ← continuation … tagFinish r₁`. `split_snd` is the program equality `Prod.snd <$> split … = RR (tag work index)`, so the final component is exactly the report run of the tagged program. `ReportSource.initialized_source` already identifies the source projection of that run with the actual initialized source execution of the tagged program, which by `tag_erase` issues exactly the requests of `work` and receives the same replies. `split_fst` shows that every countable observation of the cut component has the same distribution as under `RR (scan work index)`; the continuation is an ordinary probabilistic computation and cannot fail. `commands_initialized_map`, `reduction_map`, `initialized_map` and `run_map` are naturality in the program result, and `selected_run` gives `RR (AcceptCut.selected work index owner fallback) = mapChoice owner fallback <$> RR (scan work index)`, where `mapChoice` replaces only the program-result component by `AcceptCut.choice` and keeps local state, registry, monitor, snapshot and reporting state. `sourceResult_map` and `raw_eq` push the map through the source projection: a pending cut with raw bytes `raw` maps to the final choice `(owner, raw)` with the same Server and encoded state.

**EUF instantiation (M2).** `selected_certified` discharges the fourteen source caps of `AcceptCut.selected work index owner fallback` from `certified`. `selected_report_bound` is the address-free half of `native_origin_bound` at that program: under `capacity : (if supplied.isNone then 1 else 0) + qCreate ≤ count + 1`, the probability in `RR (selected …)` of `PqxdhRegistrationJointFinalReportEvent.Failure setup.joint codec` is at most `∑ target : Fin (count + 1), advantage setup.joint (NativeOriginBound.reduction … (selected …) … target)`. The proof is `FinalReportEvent.transport`, `FinalCandidate.reported_bound` with `ReportCosts.initialized_creates`, then `PqxdhJointPhase1Reports.fieldOrigin_advantage_le_sum`, the same chain as `native_origin_bound` after its first step. The five bills are `selected_service_bound` (at most `3 * qBundle` signing and `qFinish + qAccept` converted-DH requests per reduction), `selected_reports_bound` (at most `(count + 1) * (qAccept + 1)` public candidate reports), `selected_client_uniform` (the complete client uniform bill `total caps qInit budget`), `selected_reduction_uniform` (`32 * count + total caps qInit budget` per reduction) and `selected_game_uniform` (`32 * (count + 1) + total caps qInit budget` for the endpoint game); the uniform bills take the existing `qInit`, `initBound`, `caps` and `KemBound setup caps` premises of `NativeOriginUniform`. Each is a direct instantiation of the existing theorem with `certified` from `selected_certified`.

**Protection, classification and authentication bound (M3).** `ProtectedAt kemIndex r₁ encoded raw next value address owner target publicKey` says that the reached outcome is a pending raw cut on `raw`, that `RawSignatures.verified setup codec checks raw = some value`, that `address` locates `owner` in the public owner registry `encoded.2.2.owners`, that `owner.2.1 = kemIndex.val` (the public KEM creation index), that the owner's identity index routes to `generated target`, that the source identity key of that index is `publicKey`, that `value.identity = publicKey.ed25519`, and that `revealedAt owner.1` is false in the transcript reached at the cut. `Protected kemIndex r₁` is its existential closure. `KeyMatchCut kemIndex r₁` says that the verified PQ key of the pending candidate is the public key at index `kemIndex` of the native KEM pool at the cut; `HonestTuple` adds to protection that the verified tuple occurs in the witness owner's chronological signing history. `attached_detached` is the attached-to-detached step: from `RawSignatures.verified … raw = some value`, `RawSignatures.verified_origin` gives `candidate codec raw = some value.candidate` and `RawSignatures.verified_detached` gives `acceptPhase1Candidate setup.joint.verify value.candidate = some (value.identity, value.keys)`; the latter uses the inherited `shortRejects` premise and `setup.wiring` through `PqxdhJointPhase1Reports.source_attached_adapter`. `protected_origin` applies `AttemptReports.owner_origin` to the program `scan work index`, to the reached outcome `r₁` itself and to the witness address, with `accepted` from `attached_detached` rewritten by `value.identity = publicKey.ed25519`: either the verified tuple occurs in the owner's history and `KeyMatchCut kemIndex r₁` holds, through the owner's fixed receipt and `owner.2.1 = kemIndex.val`, or `FieldOriginFailure setup.joint ⟨target, value.candidate⟩` holds in the snapshot reached at the cut. `chosen_mapped` shows that `FinalCandidate.selected` of the mapped outcome is `some value.candidate`, by `raw_eq`, `NativeKemOrigin.selected_source` and `verified_origin`. `protected_report_failure` combines them: `Protected ∧ ¬KeyMatchCut` implies `FinalReportEvent.Failure` of `mapChoice owner fallback r₁`, whose snapshot is the one reached at the cut. `classify` (T1) shows that every supported `r₁` satisfies at least one of `CutComplete` (constructor failure, or fewer than `index + 1` raw accepts), `VerifyRejected` (attached verification `none`), `VerifiedSome ∧ ¬Protected` (attacker-owned, imported, revealed, missing owner, other identity or other KEM index, or a generated owner outside the report range (impossible under capacity)), `Protected ∧ HonestTuple ∧ KeyMatchCut` (honest unchanged or honestly re-issued tuple) and `Protected ∧ FinalReportEvent.Failure (mapChoice owner fallback r₁)` (honest identity, changed tuple). The cases are not exclusive: two generated owners with equal identity public keys can place one cut in the honest case through one owner and in the failure case through the other, and the failure case is address-free. `classify` does not separate rejection by the Server after a successful attached verification: such a cut lands in case 3, 4 or 5, as explained under "What stays open". `protected_bound` (T2) states that the probability in `RR (scan work index)` at `count + 1` positions of `Protected kemIndex ∧ ¬KeyMatchCut kemIndex` is at most `∑ target : Fin (count + 1), advantage setup.joint (NativeOriginBound.reduction … (AcceptCut.selected work index owner fallback) … target)`, by `protected_report_failure`, `selected_run` and `selected_report_bound`. There is no honest-target premise.

### Target designation and freshness reading

The target is designated by the public pair `(index, kemIndex)`: the zero-based raw-accept ordinal and the KEM creation index. The owner is located existentially in the public owner registry by address, and every comparison in `ProtectedAt` and `KeyMatchCut` is between public values: the verified identity against the owner's public identity key, the owner's public KEM index against `kemIndex`, and the verified PQ key against the public half of the native KEM pool entry. The source key lookup `sourceKeys … seeds` is an analysis projection, exactly as in `NativeKemOrigin.key_or_failure`, and is never evaluated by a reduction. No secret key is compared.

This is an intentional deviation from the recipe in the [raw-accept cut guide](registration-joint-accept-cut.md), which suggested passing a designated owner address as the owner argument of `AcceptCut.selected` and using `key_or_failure`, whose `found` premise is tied to the address `encoded.2.1.1` returned by the program. Stage A instead uses `AttemptReports.owner_origin` at the witness address and the address-free `FinalReportEvent.Failure`. `FinalCandidate.selected` reads only the raw bytes of the choice, the terminal report batch goes to all `count + 1` positions, and `FinalReportEvent.Failure` has no address, so the owner constant of `AcceptCut.selected` is never read and no owner-selection factor arises. The plan fixes that constant to `0`; the statements here are stated for an arbitrary constant `chosenOwner`, of which `0` is an instance. The fixed public `(index, kemIndex)` designation itself is owner decision 3, which the owner took on 2026-10-06 (see below). The existential owner address and the unread owner constant are owner decision 6 of the plan, which still needs the owner's confirmation.

`Protected` reads the identity freshness of the owner at the cut, in the report state reached immediately before the selected raw accept is issued (option (a) of owner decision 1 in the plan, which the owner chose on 2026-10-06). The definitions `ProtectedAt` and `Protected` are isolated in the section `CutProtection` of `PqxdhRegistrationJointAcceptOrigin`. A whole-run reading (option (b)) or a public cut-time observable recorded by the tag (option (c)) would be new predicates with their own theorems; the statements proved here would not change. The plan's stage C combines this cut-time horizon with the whole-run KEM freshness `GoodKem` (no disclosure of KEM key `kemIndex` over the whole run). The two horizons differ by design, and the planned main theorem is an event of the `split` coupling, whose final component is the report run of the tagged program; nothing in stage A states that theorem.

### Assumptions and accounting

No primitive game, contract, axiom or premise is added. The only inherited contracts are the functional attached-format premise `shortRejects` and the `setup.wiring` contract, which owner decision 4 allows in the top-level statement, both used through `RawSignatures.verified_detached`, as `KemOrigin.raw_selected` and `AttemptOrigin.raw_origin` already do. The bounds additionally take the existing creation-capacity premise `capacity` and the existing uniform-bill premises. The loss is one sum `∑ target : Fin (count + 1)` of ordinary joint-use weak-EUF advantages with coefficient one; there is no factor for the owner address, for a field or report position, or for the raw-accept ordinal, which is designated. `scan`, `selected`, `tag` and `decisionClient` forward every query exactly once, and every all-reply cap of `work` carries over; omitted continuation work is neither charged nor used to offset earlier work. A reached raw cut has `index < qAccept`, so an adversary-chosen raw ordinal would range over a family of at most `qAccept` handles; no such selection is made or charged here. No statement takes `MlKem768RecipientCorrectness`, and no KEM, Extract, HKDF or root-input statement appears, so the candidate and the KEM value enter no KDF context.

### Deviations from the plan

The probability statements use the repository's form `𝒟[(fun r => decide (P r)) <$> RR …] {true}` under `Classical.propDecidable`, as `native_origin_bound` does, instead of the plan's `Pr[P | RR …]`: the VCVio revision in use deprecates `probEvent`, `probOutput` and `evalSPMF`, and the report outcome type has no measurable-space structure. For the same reason `split_fst` is stated as an equality of the measures `𝒟` of every countable measurable observation of the cut component, which contains every cut-time event through a Boolean observation, instead of an equality of event probabilities. M2 and M3 are generic in the owner constant, as explained above. The plan's step label `rawEq` is the theorem `raw_eq`. Five helper statements are added and guarded: `finish_erase` (M0), `run_bind`, `commands_initialized_map` and `reduction_map` (M1, the last two from the scratch probe), and `selected_certified` (M2). `PqxdhRegistrationJointAcceptOrigin` imports `PqxdhRegistrationJointNativeKemOrigin` directly, because that module is outside the import closure of `PqxdhRegistrationJointAcceptCut`. No planned statement turned out false or unprovable, and none was weakened.

### What is proved

**Exposing only the selected accept (M5).** `hide setup work` interprets every exposed encapsulation of `work` in place by the actual encapsulator and leaves the result over the exposed interface. `tagged observe raw next answer` is the source continuation after the selected accept whose result is `observe (result, some (raw, answer))`. `exposeAt … observe work index owners state` runs the hidden command compiler on `AcceptCut.scan work index`; if the client completes before the selected accept it returns `observe (result, none)`; otherwise it runs the actual exposed accept on the reached native state and then the hidden command compiler on `tagged observe raw next answer`. **The KEM cut at the selected accept (M5).** `handler_eq` re-runs the scratch probe Q1 against the ported module: `CutVerification.handler` and `KemSourceSuffix.handler` are the same handler, by `rfl`. `before_callbacks` and `before_free` show that the source steps before encapsulation (freshness, reservation, both point parsers, ephemeral generation and the PQ key parse) issue no exposed request, so by `free_hide` and `scan_free_bind` they step through the KEM scan. `ordered_cut` computes the KEM cut of the literal ordered collector of one raw accept: the attached verification is the pure `RawSignatures.verified` (`CutVerification.verified_pure`); a rejected verification or a rejection before encapsulation completes without an exposed request; otherwise the cut is pending on the request `(raw, value.keys.pqKey)` with the literal interpreted suffix saved (`KemSourceSuffix.suffix_cut`). `exposeAt_scan` and `accept_scan` decompose the scan of `exposeAt` through the hidden prefix, `KemSourceSuffix.accept_ordered` and the accept tail. `rawCut_shape` states that every active cut of `exposeAt` at occurrence zero comes from a reached pending raw-accept cut `AcceptCut.Cut.pending raw next`, that `RawSignatures.verified setup codec checks raw = some value`, that the request is `(raw, value.keys.pqKey)`, and that the saved continuation is `rawResume`: the literal source suffix after the encapsulation reply, the unchanged allocation/root/consumption/publication tail of the accept, and the hidden rest of the tagged client. `accepted_reached` states that every completed cut has an outcome `observe (result, tag)` with `AcceptCutTag.accepted tag = false`: completion before the selected accept gives the tag `none`, and a rejected verification or a rejection before encapsulation gives an accept tail that returns no session (`ordered_complete`, `tail_none`). Hence, for `observe := decideTag decision`, a completed cut has decision `false`; a rejection after the hook (key conversion, DH, `server_accept` or root derivation) also ends with an accept reply `none` and therefore with `accepted = false`, by the definition of the tag.

**Raw-ordinal prechallenge (M6).** `rawScan` is the KEM scan at occurrence zero of `exposeAt` for the initialized Server, with `observe := decideTag decision`, inside the `some created` branch of the constructor exactly where `KemChallenge.pre` scans `KemEncapsulation.commands`. `preRaw` and `sourcePreRaw` are `KemChallenge.pre` and `sourcePre` with this scanned program; the failed constructor branch, the local reservations, the selected-first router and the saved context type `KemChallenge.Context KemSK A Bool count` are unchanged, so `KemResume.post`, `sourcePost`, `post_source` and `KemGame.decision` apply as they are. `preRaw_source` shows that the executable prephase under the ordinary prechallenge interpretation equals the stopped-source prephase, through the generic `PqxdhDynamicKemJointRouter.pre_program`.

**The KEM hop with the correctness error (M7).** `adversary`, `branch` and `sourceReal` are the `KemGame` definitions with the raw-ordinal prephase, and `branchRaw_source` identifies the constructed adversary's fixed branch with `branch`. `correctnessError setup` is the probability that `PqxdhKemCorrectnessError.errorExperiment setup.kem` returns `true`: one actual key pair, one encapsulation and one actual decapsulation, won by rejection or by a different secret; it is the same error experiment that HB-111 charges. `branchRaw_real_error` bounds the total variation distance between `branch … true` and `sourceReal …` by `correctnessError setup`. The proof follows `PqxdhKemCorrectnessStopped`: for each key pair, each prephase context and each challenger encapsulation, the two continuations are equal when actual decapsulation recovers the encapsulated secret (`KemResume.post_source` with the agreement given by `FunctionalDecapsulation`), and a bad encapsulation is charged in full; the bad event is then averaged over the encapsulation, the context and key generation with `PqxdhHiddenRoot.tvDist_bind_left_le_probEvent_bind` and `PqxdhKemCorrectnessStopped.error_keygen`. No program is commuted, and the single error event covers every repeated use of the challenge ciphertext by the router. `advantageRaw_error` (T3 for the plain adversary) bounds the distinguishing advantage between `sourceReal` and `branch … false` by the ordinary one-key IND-CCA advantage of the adversary plus `correctnessError setup`, through `oneKey_indCCAAdvantage_eq_branchDist` and `ProbComp.boolDistAdvantage_triangle`; `ideal_close` is its absolute-value form and `real_le_ideal` its one-sided form `𝒟[sourceReal] {true} ≤ 𝒟[branch … false] {true} + ENNReal.ofReal (Adv + correctnessError)`, which stage C needs.

**The certified adversary and its bills (M8).** The proof-carrying adversary of `KemRawCertified` saves an erased proof that its context was produced by the exact executable prephase; `prefix_erase` and `branch_erase` show that the certificate changes no query or branch, and `advantage` is T3 for this adversary. In `KemRawBills`, `Hides setup hidden exposed` says that `hidden` is `exposed` with some exposed encapsulations interpreted in place. `hides_bound` transfers a private-class cap `n` of the exposed program to `n + c * h`, for at most `h` exposed encapsulations of the exposed program and per-encapsulation cost `c`. `exposeAt_hides` shows that `exposeAt` hides every encapsulation of the fully exposed command compiler on the tagged client except the selected one, so `exposeAt_bills` gives `exposeAt` the source caps `qCreate` and `qFinish` on generated identity requests and logical decapsulations, the uniform cap `OpenUniform.total caps budget + E * qAccept`, which charges every hidden encapsulation, and one exposed encapsulation. `residual_hook` shows that after the challenge cut no exposed encapsulation remains, and `saved` turns these facts into the four continuation certificates of every valid saved context. `scan_bills` obtains the caps of the prechallenge scan through `KemCut.scan_finish` and `exposeAt_commands` from the existing actual-interpretation bills of the source, and `pre_uniform`, `pre_decapsulations` and `pre_base` follow `KemPreBills`. `uniform_cap` shows that the postchallenge allowance with the hidden encapsulations in the uniform component and no exposed encapsulation left is exactly the existing allowance, and `adversary_bound` states that the proof-carrying raw-ordinal adversary has exactly the caps of `KemReductionBills.adversary_bound`.

### Assumptions and accounting

No primitive game, contract, axiom or premise is added. The only inherited contract used in stage B is `FunctionalDecapsulation setup.kem`, which owner decision 4 allows in the top-level statement; it enters only `branchRaw_real_error`, `advantageRaw_error`, `ideal_close`, `real_le_ideal` and `KemRawCertified.advantage`, where it turns a good sample into the decapsulation agreement of `KemResume.post_source`. `shortRejects` and `setup.wiring` are not used in stage B; the attached verification enters only through `CutVerification.verified_pure`. No statement takes `MlKem768RecipientCorrectness`, and no new module cites `KemResume.supported_source`, `KemGame.branch_real`, `KemGame.advantage` or `KemCertified.advantage`; because `setup.kem` is abstract, a theorem without a correctness premise could not use them. No symbolic injectivity of ML-KEM is used. No statement mentions an Extract, HKDF or `SecretInputKdf` context, so the candidate never enters a KDF context.

The loss of the KEM step is one ordinary one-key ML-KEM IND-CCA advantage of the proof-carrying adversary, with coefficient one, plus one ordinary ML-KEM correctness error, with coefficient one; there is no selection factor, because the raw-accept ordinal and the KEM creation index are designated. Coefficient one for the error is correct because exactly one challenge sample is decapsulated against the challenger's encapsulation; every other ciphertext for key `selected` is decapsulated by the actual decapsulator in both worlds. The bills of the adversary are those of `KemReductionBills.adversary_bound`: prechallenge uniform `(qInit + 32g) + (keygenCost * count + V)`, base `decapsBase * qFinish` and `qFinish` logical decapsulations, and postchallenge uniform `V`, base `decapsBase * qFinish` and `qFinish` logical decapsulations, with `V = 32 * qCreate + (OpenUniform.total caps budget + E * qAccept) + Du * qFinish`, where `E = caps.encapsCost`, `Du = caps.decapsUniform` and `g` is 1 when the Server identity is generated and 0 otherwise. The hidden encapsulations are charged in the uniform component: `OpenUniform.total` already contains `(96 + E) * qAccept`, and `V` adds `E * qAccept` again, so the totals do not change; the certificate components do, with uniform `OpenUniform.total + E * qAccept` and no exposed encapsulation after the cut. The separate phase caps conservatively sum to at most `2 * qFinish` logical decapsulations; no tighter shared counter is claimed. The challenger adds one key generation, one encapsulation and one 32-byte ghost draw; the correctness error needs no adversary bill.

### Deviations from the plan

`exposeAt` is generic in the observation `observe : X × Tag → R` of the tagged result instead of taking the decision `D : X → Bool`; the plan's program is the instance `observe := decideTag D`, for which `observe <$> tag work index` is `decisionClient D work index`. The generic form serves the stage C naturality step M9a and changes no statement for the Boolean instance. `exposeAt` also takes the identity index, the public identity, the owner registry and the native state as `KemEncapsulation.commands` does, and `rawScan` instantiates them with the initialized Server as `KemChallenge.pre` does. `rawCut_shape` states the saved continuation in its literal form `rawResume`; the form through `KemOpaqueResume.resume_reply` needs lane port L2 and belongs to stage D. `accepted_reached` is stated on the KEM cut (a completed cut has a non-accepted tag), the contrapositive reading of the plan's "the `i`-th accept returns a session only if the hook was pending". The plan's T3 is `KemRawCertified.advantage` for the proof-carrying adversary; `KemRawGame.advantageRaw_error` is the same bound for the plain adversary. The plan's `εKEM` is the definition `KemRawGame.correctnessError`, written with the measure `𝒟[…] {true}` because the VCVio revision in use deprecates `probOutput` and the `Pr[= …]` notation. The plan's M8 `post_bound` is `adversary_post`, which uses the existing context-generic `KemPostBills.post_bound` directly, and its `pre_bills` are `pre_uniform`, `pre_decapsulations` and `pre_base`. M8 is split into `KemRawCertified` and `KemRawBills`, as the plan allows. The added helper statements are listed in the module table; `real_le_ideal` is added for stage C. No planned statement turned out false or unprovable, and none was weakened.

## What stays open

`classify` of stage A does not separate rejection after a successful attached verification. In `Server::get_shared_secret` (`beaconcrypt/src/server.rs`), the steps after `validate_init_kex` can still reject: the consumed-registration status check, `try_reserve`, the prekey and one-time key parses, ephemeral key generation, the PQ key parse, encapsulation, the key conversions and four DH steps, `shared_secrets`, `server_accept` and `derive_root_key_input`. A cut that the Server rejects at one of these steps lands in case 3, 4 or 5 of `classify`; a replayed honest registration, for example, satisfies case 4 although the Server rejects it as consumed. Stage B proves the KEM-side half: `accepted_reached` shows that a rejection before encapsulation gives a completed KEM cut with `accepted = false`, and a rejection after the hook ends with the accept reply `none` and therefore `accepted = false` by the definition of the tag. The classification that combines `classify` with this rejection class is `classify_final` (stage C).

Stage C (modules M4, M9a, M9 and M10): key-list monotonicity along the continuation, KEM-side client-result naturality, the bridge from the actual source to the raw KEM real game (`good_source`, `active_raw`, `coins_stopped`, `router_split`, `real_split`, `actual_le_real`), and, in M10, the main theorem T5 with its bills companion T5b and the final classification `classify_final`; T5 and T5b combine `split`, `split_snd`, `split_fst`, `protected_bound`, `KemRawGame.real_le_ideal` and `KemRawBills.adversary_bound`. Stage D (lane port L2 and module M11): the opaque form of the KEM-ideal continuation, including `rawCut_shape` through `KemOpaqueResume.resume_reply`. Owner decisions 2, 5 and 6 of the plan remain open, in particular the confirmation of the existential owner address; decisions 1, 3 and 4 are recorded above. Handoff item 2 (the Extract/HKDF hybrid at the actual joint sampler, including context secrecy of the candidate), items 3 to 8 and the final validation matrix are untouched. Persistence, snapshots, attacker access to running code and codec internals remain outside the experiment, and primitive implementations remain trusted.
