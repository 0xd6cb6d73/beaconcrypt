# Record-lift CTX privacy half: random target, three-mode challenger, link, hybrids and bills (items 4 and 5, stage S4)

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxRandom`, `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivModes`, `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivLink`, `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPriv` and `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivBills` are the five modules of the privacy half of `ctx_lift` (stage S4 of the takeover plan, decision D1). `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivBills.ctxPrivLiftStatement` proves `PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.CtxPrivLiftStatement` exactly as defined on central (both forms) for the explicit reduction family `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPriv.privReduction` and the explicit bills `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivBills.privBills`: in either observation mode, `idealAuth` (every hidden coordinate in replay mode) and `randomRecords` (every hidden coordinate in random mode; in mode P this is `idealRecords`) differ, for every cache-free decision and every Boolean event, by at most the exact sum over the `hops n budget` target key indices of `privacyLoss`, the ordinary modified-nonce AEAD IND$ advantage of `AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.viewReduction` of the hop's one-key adversary, each with coefficient one, and every hop's adversary respects the bills. Along the way `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxRandom` closes the open point of the S1 targets: random mode (`PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.randomImpl`) is the IND$ random experiment behind `AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.replay_random_advantage`. The five modules have 30, 32, 11, 14 and 26 guarded theorems (113 in total). No theorem has an assumption or contract hypothesis; the contracts of the brief's H-7 list that they use are listed per theorem in the section [Contracts per theorem](#contracts-per-theorem). Commands, exit statuses and digests are recorded in the receipt [`doc/impl/proof-receipts/record-lift-priv.json`](proof-receipts/record-lift-priv.json).

## The random one-key target is the IND$ random experiment

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxRandom.randomTarget decode` runs `randomImpl` on the shared BLAKE2b cache, exactly as the replay one-key target `AdaptiveCtxSharedModes.targetImpl false` runs replay mode (`randomTarget_keyImpl`, `replayTarget_keyImpl`: both are `PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.keyImpl` in their mode, on the shared cache). Through the replay mode's state projection it is the receive-state interpreter `randomReplayImpl`, request by request (`seal_interpretation`, `local_interpretation`, `target_step`) and for every program (`target_schedule`, `randomTargetView_eq`): coins, independent public hashing, `randomReceiveSeal` (nonce refusal; otherwise a uniform body of the plaintext's length, a uniform 16-byte tag, and the commitment from the key-free suffix dictionary) and replay-dictionary raw acceptance (`AdaptiveCtxReplayDictionary.replayRaw`).

`AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.randomView decode client` is the modified-nonce AEAD IND$ random experiment run on `viewReduction decode client`: the client behind the public replay guard (`guardedClient`), compiled by the key-free CTX wrapper `AdaptiveCtxReceive.wrapped` and the raw-log simulator `AdaptiveCtxLogSimulation.runLogged`. `runLogged_eq` writes the log simulator as a stateful interpreter (`logImpl`); `randomLogged` links it to the IND$ random challenger and `randomLinked` adds the wrapper. `linked_step` shows, request by request and without an invariant premise, that the composite is the direct logged random source `loggedRandomImpl` with the challenger's log and nonce history determined by the receive state (`lift`); `linked_schedule` extends this to every program (through the embedding lemma `run_simulateQ_embed`). The guard removes itself on the logged random source: its recorded history is the actual successful-seal history (`recordReply_history`), and its raw replies are the replay dictionary's (`AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.guardedRaw_eq_replay`), so `guard_query` and `guard_run` give the random replay source. `randomView_eq` concludes that `randomView` is exactly (as a probabilistic program, not only in law) the random replay source's decision, and `randomTargetView_randomView` that it is the random one-key target's decision. With `AdaptiveCtxSharedModes.replay_view_eq` and `AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.replay_random_advantage`, `replay_random_target` gives the per-key hop: the replay and random one-key targets differ, for every one-key client, by exactly the IND$ advantage of `viewReduction`, so `privacyLoss` is the replay-to-random distance of the one-key targets (`privacyLoss_eq`). The module also proves the forwarding and public-history laws of the random target (`random_forward`, `forward_step`, `forward_complete`, `local_public_history`, `public_history_step`) that `AdaptiveCtxSharedModes` proves for real and replay mode.

## The three-mode challenger and the sampler swap

The privacy hybrids put coordinates in replay or random mode, so the one-key reduction of a hop must run the other coordinates' records in random mode too, which the real/replay known calls of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModes` cannot. `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivModes.KnownCall` carries a `KeyMode`; `knownLocal sample` runs real and replay mode with the call's key and random mode as `randomWith sample`, `randomImpl` with its body and tag drawn by a byte-string sampler. With the world's sampler `uniformBytes` (one coin per byte) a known call is exactly `keyImpl` in its mode (`randomWith_uniform`, `knownLocal_uniform`). `modeTarget selected` is the one-key target of any mode on the shared cache (`modeTarget_replay`, `modeTarget_random`), with the forwarding and public-history laws in every mode (`mode_forward_complete`, `mode_public_history_step`). As in `AdaptiveCtxDynamicKnownKeys`, `sourceImpl` is the challenger's source semantics and `reductionImpl` the key-free reduction (`selected_step`, `known_step`, `linked_step`, `linked_schedule`, `schedule_interpretation`); `source_view` identifies the challenger's game decision with the constructed ordinary one-key client `decisionClient` against the selected target (`modeView`), which is `replayView` in replay mode (`modeView_replay`) and has the law of `randomView` in random mode (`modeView_random`; the selected key is drawn but unused).

A random-mode known seal draws a body of the plaintext's length. With one coin per byte the constructed client's coin count would grow with plaintext lengths, which no `Budget` field bounds, so no bill could be stated in `BillInput`. `bytesSample` draws a whole byte string with one coin query (`bytesSample_eq`) and has the same uniform law (`bytesSample_evalDist`). `AdaptiveCtxQueryBounds.SourceUniform`, the class that the uniform cap of `CtxBounded` counts, caps the number of coin queries, each a uniform draw from a finite range of any size, as in every existing bill, so a uniform byte string of any length costs one query. `SameLaw left right` says that the two computations give the same law of every Boolean decision; it is a congruence for bind and for stateful simulation (`sameLaw_bind`, `sameLaw_simulateQ`), the two samplers satisfy it (`sameLaw_bytes`), hence so do random seals (`seal_run`, `sameLaw_seal`), known calls (`sameLaw_known`), the challenger's requests (`sameLaw_source_step`) and its whole game (`sameLaw_sourceGame`). The sampler swap is stated with the measure `𝒟`. Besides it, two steps of the privacy half are equalities of distributions rather than of programs: `modeView_random`, which drops the selected key that random mode draws but never reads (`evalDist_bind_const`), and the selected-key law `PqxdhRecordLiftKeyCut.key_cut_measure` of the integrity half, through which `game_replay` and `game_random` hold (below). The other identifications of targets, views, games and worlds (`randomView_eq`, `randomTargetView_randomView`, `source_view`, `modeView_replay`, `link`, `game_first`, `game_last`) are program equalities, and the hop equalities (`replay_random_target`, `hop_advantage`, `hop_eq`) are equalities of advantages obtained from these through `AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.replay_random_advantage` and `PqxdhRecordLiftCtx.advantage_congr`. With the world's sampler on the challenger's side and the single-coin sampler inside the constructed client, the replay game has the law of the replay view (`replay_game`) and the random game the law of the IND$ random experiment (`random_game`), so `hop_advantage`: the two games differ by exactly the IND$ advantage of `viewReduction` of the constructed client.

## The link

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivLink.reduction target modes` is the reduction of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxLink` with one change: every hidden coordinate other than the selected one is routed as a known call in its own mode (`routeKeyed`), random mode included. The explicit-key worlds `ctxRunWith`, the counter-erased CTX interpreter, the slot code `slotOf`, the projections `keysOf` and `linkProj` are reused from `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxLink`. `linked_step` and `linked_schedule` show, for every service and every state and without a reachability or mode premise, that the reduction run against the challenger's source state with the world's sampler realizes the counter-erased CTX world with the selected key supplied externally and the selected coordinate in the challenger's mode (`selectedModesM`). `link` concludes: the challenger's game in mode `sel` against the key-free client `linkClient` is the CTX world in which the selected coordinate's key is a fresh uniform key in mode `sel` and every other hidden coordinate keeps its table key and its mode.

## Hybrids, hops and telescope

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPriv.privModes positions cut` puts a coordinate in random mode exactly when `PqxdhRecordLiftChainOrder.selected positions cut` selects it, and in replay mode otherwise: the hybrid order and the key cut of the integrity half, with replay in place of real and random in place of replay. The first hybrid is `idealAuth` (`privModes_zero`, `game_first`); after all `hops n budget` steps exactly the bounded coordinates are in random mode (`privModes_last`), and since no CTX world reaches any other coordinate (`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxAvailability.ctxRunWith_congr`) the last hybrid is `randomRecords` (`game_last`). One step changes only its own coordinate, from replay to random (`privModes_unchanged`, `privModes_step`). For step `step`, `stepClient` is the link client of the step's coordinate `entryAt step` under the modes before the step, and `privReduction` is the explicit CTX reduction family: the step's ordinary one-key adversary is `decisionClient` of the step client with the single-coin sampler. By the selected-key law `PqxdhRecordLiftKeyCut.key_cut_measure` (which holds for every mode assignment) and the link, the hybrid before the step has the law of the challenger's replay game and the hybrid after it that of the challenger's random game (`game_replay`, `game_random`, with `selectedModesM_replay` and `selectedModesM_random`), so each hop is exactly the step's IND$ advantage (`hop_eq`), that is its `privacyLoss` (`hop_loss`). `ctxPrivLiftBool_of_billed` telescopes the hops with `PqxdhComposition.finiteHybridBound_of_hop_bounds` (coefficient one) and proves `CtxPrivLiftBool` for every bill record from `StepBilled bills` (every step's adversary is `CtxBounded` by the bills); `ctxPrivLiftStatement_of_billed` gives both forms through `PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal.ctxPrivLiftStatement_of_bool`.

## The bills

`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivBills` proves `StepBilled privBills` (`stepBilled`) and hence `ctxPrivLiftStatement`. Local bounds: random mode with the single-coin sampler draws one coin for the body and one for the tag (`bytesSample_total`) plus at most 64 for the private commitment fill, and no shared hash for a seal (`seal_uniform`, `seal_hash`, `random_uniform_step`, `random_hash_step`); with `AdaptiveCtxSharedReplayBounds` for real and replay mode, every known call draws one coin per coin request, at most 66 per keyed request, and makes at most one shared-hash request per non-coin request (`known_uniform_step`, `known_hash_step`). Through the three-mode reduction (`uniform_step`, `hash_step`, `target_step`), `decision_budgets` turns the route ledgers of a three-mode client (`UniformRoute`, `KeyedRoute`, `HashRoute`, `TargetClass`) into the constructed one-key client's coin, shared-hash, selected-seal and selected-raw bounds. Per service, the privacy link reduction forwards each coin and public hash as one request (`coinForward_route`, `hashForward_route`), routes at most one request per hidden opening or seal (`opening_route`, `sealing_route`) and makes at most one hash per caller-known send or receive; `reduction_step` and its instances give the five ledgers (`reduction_uniform`, `reduction_keyed`, `reduction_hash`, `reduction_seal`, `reduction_raw`), and `link_route` adds the decision's coins. The program-level ledgers of the integrity bills (`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxBills.program_categories`, `program_target_seal`, `programBill`) are reused unchanged. `privBills` charges the program's coin bill plus the decision's coins plus 66 coins per keyed request (bounded by the shared-hash ledger, which counts every hidden opening and seal), the program's shared hashes (the world's shared-hash total, `hash_bill`), one seal (the target coordinate is sealed at most once, so the seal cap of the bills is 1) and `Budget.receives` raw requests. The bills are explicit but not tight; the target requires only that they are functions of `BillInput`.

## Contracts per theorem

No theorem of the five modules has an assumption or contract hypothesis: the premises of the main theorems are the adversary's budget, the environment's `Frame`, the decision's coin bound and `StepBilled bills`, which are budget facts and properties of the explicit construction. Which contracts of the brief's H-7 list a theorem uses was determined by a closure probe over the constant dependencies of all 113 theorems (`ContractProbe.lean` in the scratch tools, recorded in the receipt). No theorem uses `Pqxdh.Crypto.aead_open_seal`, `SourceFullCorrect`, `FrameReadBack`, `DecoderAgreement`, the AEAD history-correctness predicates, HMAC-PRF or Extract-on-`uniform32`.

- Functional AEAD correctness, tag length only: 23 theorems use the field `Pqxdh.Crypto.aeadSeal_tag_length` (16-byte tags) in their proofs, all through central lemmas about the replay side: `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxRandom.replay_random_target` and `privacyLoss_eq` (through `AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.replay_random_advantage`, whose real-view simulation uses it); `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivModes.mode_public_history_step`, `selected_step`, `linked_step`, `linked_schedule`, `schedule_interpretation`, `source_view`, `modeView_replay`, `replay_game`, `random_game` and `hop_advantage` (through `AdaptiveCtxSharedModes.public_history_step` and `replay_view_eq`); `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPriv.hop_eq`, `hop_loss`, `ctxPrivLiftBool_of_billed` and `ctxPrivLiftStatement_of_billed`; `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivBills.known_hash_step`, `known_uniform_step`, `uniform_step`, `hash_step`, `decision_budgets`, `stepBilled` and `ctxPrivLiftStatement` (through the replay-mode bounds of `AdaptiveCtxSharedReplayBounds`). 28 further theorems reach it only inside the AEAD record-building definitions (structural).
- The modified-nonce AEAD IND$ game enters only as the advantage term of `privacyLoss` in `replay_random_target`, `privacyLoss_eq`, `hop_advantage`, `hop_eq`, `hop_loss`, `ctxPrivLiftBool_of_billed`, `ctxPrivLiftStatement_of_billed` and `ctxPrivLiftStatement`; `randomView_eq` and `randomTargetView_randomView` are equalities with its random experiment and use no game assumption.
- The BLAKE2b random-oracle model is the world model: every world and one-key target shares one lazily sampled BLAKE2b cache.
- Structural fields only: the DH-interface fields `dh_comm`, `xpub_length` and `x25519_length` (22 theorems, through the type of the `setup` parameter, classified by the H-7 closure guard) and `hkdf_length` (22 theorems, through the KDF definition `RawCtxSourceHkdf.response` behind `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxProtocol.knownRom`).
- Every other theorem uses no contract.

This is not a new hypothesis: `aeadSeal_tag_length` is a field of the primitive record `setup.source.crypto` that every statement takes as a parameter, classified (as for the integrity half) under functional AEAD correctness, and `doc/composition-assumptions.md` already lists functional AEAD correctness, the modified-nonce AEAD IND$ game and the BLAKE2b random-oracle model for the one-key endpoints used here.

## Deviations from the plan

1. The task named `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModeBounds.selected_privacy` for the per-key hops. That bound compares the real one-key game with `randomView` and charges the integrity terms as well as the IND$ term; the target's privacy hybrids start from `idealAuth` (replay mode) and charge `privacyLoss` only, so the hop is `replay_random_advantage` (exact, no integrity term), as the CTX-track notes had already found. The hybrid order and the key cut are those of the integrity half.
2. The open point of the S1 targets needed a full simulation of the IND$ random experiment (module `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxRandom`), not a one-line correspondence: the random challenger is composed with the raw-log simulator, the key-free wrapper and the public guard.
3. The link needed known calls in random mode, so the three-mode challenger (`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivModes`) and a privacy variant of the link (`PqxdhRecordLiftCtxPrivLink`) are new modules rather than reuses of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModes` and `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxLink`.
4. New obligation found and proved: a random-mode known seal with the world's sampler draws one coin per plaintext byte, so its coins have no bill in `BillInput`; the constructed clients use the single-coin sampler and the sampler swap `sameLaw_sourceGame` (a law equality under every Boolean decision) relates them to the world.
5. Module split: five modules (random target, three-mode challenger, link, telescope, bills).
