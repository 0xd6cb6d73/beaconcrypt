# Serial ProVerif groups in continuous verification

The legacy ProVerif matrix remains unchanged. Two additional CI jobs invoke the existing serial free-schedule group and the separate extension group. The first runs registration, server-to-beacon records and beacon-to-server records in their existing order. The second runs the primitive encoding profile, active strong-secrecy experiments, moved-pending registration controls, open CTX differential controls, the separately reviewed A2 signature-provenance projection and A1 delayed-response projection, also in order. No target joins the legacy parallel roster, and no existing result, model, query or verdict classification changes.

Each wrapper enters the pinned proof shell, checks the toolchain, regenerates the ProVerif extraction, and invokes the maintained gates. After those gates finish it rejects both tracked and untracked extraction drift. The old generated-scenario checks remain intact. Transcript-fidelity and verdict-classification checks remain prerequisites of the individual gates, so the CI wrapper cannot replace a proof result with a metadata result.

Individual verifier runs retain a ceiling of 1,800 seconds and 10 GiB address space; the pending-token group keeps its stricter 180-second default and provenance and delayed response enforce a 180-second maximum. The original fs group retains its approximately three-hour proof budget. Its CI job allows 210 minutes overall to include checkout, tool installation, extraction and test overhead. The extension job has a separate 180-minute overall allowance; neither job enlarges a verifier's per-target limit. These are CI wall-clock limits, not cryptographic query budgets or permission to reinterpret a timeout as evidence. Any nonzero gate exit fails its job, and the unchanged required aggregate CI check depends on the complete matrix.

The source-level wiring check verifies the exact sequential invocation lists, locked-shell dispatch, existence of every referenced gate, generated-drift rejection and both CI entries. Negative cases omit strong secrecy, registration provenance or a CI entry, fork extension checks, bypass the drift step, ignore tracked differences or drop untracked-file detection. The existing fs tests continue to check the old serial roster and per-target caps. These focused checks validate wiring only; the final stepwise proof matrix still establishes the actual verdicts on the integration result.

No production Rust, primitive contract, source abstraction, freshness rule or security theorem changes. Historical failed-receive fixtures and the baseline/active-classical duplicate remain checked with their explicit classifications; this checkpoint removes no legacy query or gate. Snapshots and persistence remain outside the proof objective.

Two pre-existing reference links in the maintained analysis now resolve to their actual headings: the fixed-array timing limitation in the historical Stage 4 record, and the persistence overview. This repairs navigation only; snapshots and persistence remain excluded from the current proof scope.

The A2 reconciliation appends its six already checked targets to the extension recipe and exact source-level roster. It preserves the reviewed source/model bytes and archived results, including the distinction between the origin implication and auxiliary identity-secrecy checks. The existing overall CI allowance is unchanged.

The A1 addition runs its five small delayed-response targets after provenance in the same serial extension job, with unchanged overall allowance. Exact source-level wiring and negative omission cases retain its checker and regression gates; its seven classified verdict lines keep their separate scopes.

The exact-far addition appends five literal-material targets after delayed response in the extension recipe.
