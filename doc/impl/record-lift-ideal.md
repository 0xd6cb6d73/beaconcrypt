# Record-lift ideal worlds and target statements (items 4 and 5, stage S1)

`PqxdhRecordLiftIdeal` fixes the targets of handoff items 4 and 5 before they are proved: the ideal worlds `idealChains`, `idealAuth` and `idealRecords`, the permitted decision view, and the H-5 statements of the record-lift brief (`/tmp/bc-coord/tasks/codex-items-4-5.md`) as `Prop`-valued definitions. It proves none of the targets. Its 21 guarded theorems only show that the definitions are well-formed and fit together. The module is stage S1 of the takeover plan (`/tmp/bc-arc-scratch/items45-takeover/takeover-plan.md` §2); the chain track (S3) and the CTX track (S4) are proved against these targets, and CE-12 consumes them.

## The worlds

All worlds run the same key-free compiled client, `PqxdhRecordLiftProtocol.program`: the frozen interface program (`PqxdhRecordLiftInterface.program`, with the CE ledger wrapper and the mode projection `asModeO`) compiled to the ten private services of `PqxdhRecordLiftProtocol.ServiceSpec`. The worlds differ only in the private interpreter of those services.

`chains ideal` interprets the client with the mixed lookup policy `PqxdhRecordLiftMixedWorld.policy setup.source ideal`. A coordinate (slot, direction, position) with `ideal` false calls the actual native HKDF; a coordinate with `ideal` true reads the persistent lazy full76 table, which keeps its entries across rollback and re-derivation. `realChains` (every coordinate real) is the frozen record world: `realChains_analysis` shows that on the CE-12 analysis projection it is `PqxdhRecordLiftWorld.analysis <$> PqxdhRecordLiftWorld.run`, the projection that CE-11's C1 join `PqxdhDeploymentEnvironmentJoin.join_analysis` equates with the native deployment game, and `realChains_game` shows the same for `PqxdhRecordLiftWorld.game`. `chains_selected_zero` shows that index zero of the ordered hybrid of `PqxdhRecordLiftChainOrder` is `realChains`. `idealChains` (every coordinate ideal) is the brief's chain-ideal world (H-4).

`ctxRun modes replace` keeps `idealChains`' interpreter for eight of the ten services (`ctxInterpreter_base`: allocation, coins, public hashes, charges, table lookups, known-kernel sends and receives, publication) and changes only the two hidden-record services. A hidden opening (`hiddenOpening`) performs the source payload-parser rejection, the actual total getters on chains read from the table and the 153-byte associated-data check, then makes one keyed request `(coordinate, raw)` with the universal envelope that `RawCtxUniversalOpening.call` uses for a selected key, and returns the source API reply. A hidden sealing (`hiddenSealing`) performs the associated-data check, makes one keyed seal request with the selected input of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxServices.sealCall`, logs the frame before the writer result and returns the source API reply. A malformed payload or a wrong associated-data width makes no keyed request (`hiddenOpening_malformed`, `hiddenSealing_narrow`). The keyed request of coordinate `c` is answered (`keyed`) by the one-key CTX local state of `c` in mode `modes c`, with the key and nonce of `c`'s table entry (`materialAt_native`: the native material of the same entry), the world's shared BLAKE2b cache and its counted coins.

The three modes (`keyImpl`): `real` is `AdaptiveCtxSharedModes.localImpl true`; `replay` is `AdaptiveCtxSharedModes.localImpl false`, which seals with the real AEAD, commits through a private suffix dictionary and accepts a raw frame only if it is in the coordinate's replay dictionary of successful seals; `random` (`randomImpl`, `randomSeal`) is the replay mode with a uniformly random body of the plaintext's length and a uniformly random 16-byte tag, the seal semantics of the ordinary IND$ random experiment. `keyImpl_modes` records that real and replay are exactly the two endpoints of `PqxdhRecordLiftCtxModeBounds.selected_integrity`, and `randomImpl_other` that random mode differs from replay mode only on sealing. A hidden seal that repeats a nonce at its coordinate is refused (reply none, no frame); the native seal never refuses, so this is the explicit refusal that the refusal terms charge, and it never happens because every hidden sealing coordinate is sealed at most once (`PqxdhRecordLiftWorldProvenance.tracedProgram_nodup`).

`idealAuth` puts every hidden coordinate in replay mode. `idealRecords` puts every hidden coordinate in replay mode in observation mode O and in random mode in observation mode P (`idealRecords_O`, `idealRecords_P`); its replies are mode-projected by the unchanged `asModeO`. `randomRecords` puts every hidden coordinate in random mode in either observation mode. The plaintext map `replace` is the identity in every world except the hiding comparison.

## The view

`View` is the permitted decision view of a completed run: the adversary's output, the world-owned ledger (transcript with mode-O replies, the test session with its bit, the fresh messages), the environment's own final state, the tagged produced-frame log, the raw-attempt counters and the handle-to-slot map of installed hidden kernels (`handleSlot`, read from the stored record objects). It contains no seed, chain table, coordinate label, kernel byte, CTX dictionary, primitive counter or shared cache. The CE-12 analysis projection factors through it (`analysis_chainView`, `View.analysis`). The environment state and the slot map are additions beyond the analysis projection: the first is needed by the later-quantum post phase (R6), the second by the origin event; both make the targets stronger. Chain decisions may also read the final shared BLAKE2b cache; every other decision is cache-free, because commitment preimages contain message keys (decision D4).

## The targets

Every lift target has a Boolean form (`...Bool`: the `boolDistAdvantage` of the two worlds under a decision with coin cost `cost`) and an event form (`...Event`: the absolute difference of the probabilities of a Boolean event), and the statement is their conjunction (`...Statement`). The event form follows from the Boolean form (`chainLiftStatement_of_bool`, `ctxAuthLiftStatement_of_bool`, `ctxPrivLiftStatement_of_bool`, `recordLiftStatement_of_bool`, through `pure_advantage`). Each lift is quantified over every environment with `Frame env startup allowance` and every adversary with `budget.Respects adversary`, the premises that CE-11 discharges (C2a and the adversary budget). Each loss is an exact sum with coefficient one over the `hops n budget = positions budget * (n * 2)` ordered hybrid indices of `PqxdhRecordLiftChainOrder`, where `positions budget = sends + 51 * receives` is the frozen `Caps.derivations` (`positions_derivations`); the same indices enumerate target keys. Reductions are explicit parameters (`ChainReduction`, `CtxReduction`: one reduction per index, built from the environment, the adversary, the decision and the budget), and every reduction must respect a bill that is a function of `BillInput` (the budget fields, the environment's `Frame` data, the fresh bounds, the slot count and the decision cost).

`ChainLiftStatement` (item 5, `chain_lift`): `realChains` and `idealChains` differ, for every decision on the view and the final shared cache, by at most the sum of `hopLoss` over the indices (Extract on `uniform32` plus HMAC-PRF for each index's full76 observer, the right side of `PqxdhRecordLiftGlobalCut.observed_bound` and `PqxdhRecordLiftGlobalSeedCut.observed_bound`) plus the aliasing and refusal terms. Contract: `SourceFullCorrect`.

`CtxAuthLiftStatement` (item 4, integrity half, decision D1): `idealChains` and `idealAuth` differ, in either observation mode and for every cache-free decision, by at most the sum of `integrityLoss` over the indices (the four terms of `selected_integrity` for each index's one-key adversary at its hash and raw bills: IND$ validation, `qHash/2^128`, twice the attempt-indexed INT-CTXT sum, `qRaw/2^512`) plus the refusal term.

`CtxPrivLiftStatement` (item 4, privacy half, decision D1): `idealAuth` and `randomRecords` differ, in either observation mode and for every cache-free decision, by at most the sum of `privacyLoss` over the indices (the IND$ advantage of `AdaptiveCtxPrivacyGuard.viewReduction`, which equals the replay-to-random distance of one key by `replay_random_advantage`). In mode P the right world is `idealRecords`; in mode O it is the IND$ step of the origin target.

`RecordLiftStatement` (`record_lift`): `realChains` and `idealRecords` differ, for every cache-free decision, by at most `recordLoss`: the chain loss at the decision that ignores the cache, the aliasing and both refusal terms, the integrity loss and, in mode P, the privacy loss. `recordLiftBool_of_parts` proves that the three parts give it by the triangle inequality, every term with coefficient one. Contract: `SourceFullCorrect`.

`OriginStatement` (`idealRecords_origin`): in `idealAuth` and `idealRecords`, in both observation modes, the probability that some accepted raw receive on a handle holding hidden slot `j` carries a frame that no handle of the opposite role holding `j` ever produced is zero (`UnproducedAccept`, the replay-dictionary invariant); the probability of CE-OR's event (`UnpublishedAccept`: the frame was not returned by such a handle earlier in the transcript) is at most `guessTerm budget = R * S / 2^128` in `randomRecords` and at most `publicationTerm` in `idealRecords`, which is `guessTerm` in mode P and `guessTerm` plus the IND$ sum of the privacy half at the origin event in mode O (decision D2, owner input 9). Contracts: `FrameReadBack` (a returned ciphertext reads back as its frame) and `DecoderAgreement` (Server routing agrees with the frame's sender).

`HidingStatement` (`idealRecords_hiding`, mode P): replacing every hidden seal plaintext by one of equal length does not change the law of `hidingView`, the mode-P public projection that keeps the secrecy ground truth (output, the mode-P transcript view, the test session, the fresh messages with their replies, the environment state, produced frames, counters and slot map). `hiding_advantage` and `hiding_event` give the Boolean and event forms with advantage zero. No contract.

`ChainLiftTarget`, `CtxAuthLiftTarget` and `RecordLiftTarget` fix the aliasing and refusal terms to zero (`noTerm`, decision D3). The structural reasons, which the delivering stages must prove and the reviewer must confirm: routing is by coordinate and never by key or chain bytes, the seed kernels are total, install refusals do not depend on seeds, and each hidden sealing coordinate is sealed at most once, so no per-key nonce refusal occurs.

A target with free reduction parameters is a statement shape. An artificial reduction (for example an unbounded observer with advantage close to one) satisfies it trivially, so a target is a deliverable only together with the explicit simulator-built reductions and closed-form bills of the theorem that proves it, and those are reviewed with that theorem.
