## What is proved

Notation: codec′ is `PqxdhBoundaryRecordPrelude.withReader codec reader` (the abstract codec with its frame reader replaced by the production decoder `RawCtxFrameDecoding.decode reader.model`), route′ is `RawCtxFrameDecoding.sender reader.model` (the production header router), "the classical source game" is `PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.sourceGame setup codec reader erased checks ensureInit 0 bounds mode adversary` and "the LQ-F source game" is `PqxdhBoundaryDeploymentLater.laterSourceGame setup codec reader erased checks ensureInit 0 bounds mode first second`, both at the Server identity index 0 that every CE-12 headline uses. The PB-D modules prove that these source games, which run the Lean source programs transcribed from the Rust adapter functions, are equal to the CE and LQ-F games at codec′ and route′ on their full returned value up to the Rust success wrapper (`classical`, `later`, `classical_zero`, `later_zero`, ledger rows PB-01 to PB-14).

`PqxdhDeploymentProductionTransfer` (8 theorems) holds the generic part. `observe_zero` and `evalDist_zero` state, for every observation of the CE game's returned value, that the observation read back through `PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.unwrap` on the classical source game is the same computation (and has the same output distribution) as the observation on `PqxdhDeploymentSecrecy.game setup codec′ checks route′ ensureInit 0 bounds mode adversary` (through `classical_zero`, `unwrap_okMap` and `classical_evalDist`); `chance` is the Boolean-event form `𝒟[(decision ∘ unwrap) <$> source] {true} = 𝒟[decision <$> game] {true}`. `observe_later_zero`, `evalDist_later_zero` and `chance_later` are the same for the LQ-F game `PqxdhDeploymentLaterServerGame.game … first second` and the LQ-F source game (through `later_zero` and `later_evalDist`). `frameReadBack_withReader` and `readWritable_withReader` state what the two codec contracts that some headlines take say at codec′ (by `Iff.rfl`); they are readings of carried premises, not discharges. `unwrap` reads the Rust `fail` and `div` results as `none`; the source games never produce them (`classical_normal`, `later_normal`).

The three headline modules restate frozen CE-12 theorems, keeping each frozen name. For a frozen theorem with binders `Bs` and a conclusion that mentions the CE (or LQ-F) game, the production form keeps `Bs` in order, adds `(reader : RawCtxNativeFramePrelude.Reader Message Root)` and `(erased : Aeneas.Std.Array Aeneas.Std.U8 229#usize)` after `codec`, drops `route`, keeps `Phase1ReadBack codec` and `Phase2ReadBack codec`, and puts codec′ for `codec` and route′ for `route` everywhere else; every block `𝒟[F <$> (PqxdhDeploymentSecrecy.game setup codec checks route ensureInit 0 bounds mode A).run runtime]` of the conclusion becomes `𝒟[(F′ ∘ PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.unwrap) <$> (PqxdhBoundaryDeployment.sourceGame setup codec reader erased checks ensureInit 0 bounds mode A).run runtime]`, with `F′` the frozen event at codec′ and route′ (LQ-F blocks likewise with `laterSourceGame` and `PqxdhBoundaryDeploymentLater.unwrap`), and the rest of the conclusion (losses, ideal games, record worlds, explicit terms, bills) is the frozen text at codec′ and route′. For `ct_collision_game`, `ct_coll_le` and `ct_coll_billed`, whose left side is the explicit term `ctCollTerm`, that term is first unfolded to its definition (the probability of `ctCollEvent` on the CE game) and then moved. Each proof is `simp only [PqxdhDeploymentProductionTransfer.chance]` (or `chance_later`) followed by the frozen theorem applied at codec′ and route′, with the read-back laws transported by `PqxdhBoundaryDeploymentStep.phase1_withReader` and `phase2_withReader` (`Iff.rfl`). So each production form has the frozen bound word for word at the production instance, and its probabilities are probabilities of the production source game. ## What the production forms say, and what they do not

The left side of every production form is an event of the production source game; the right side is the frozen CE-12 bound at the production codec and route. The bound's terms are CE-model quantities at that instance: advantages of the reductions that CE-12 builds (KEM, key-schedule, record-lift, signature and collision reductions), probabilities of CE's ideal games and record worlds, the anomaly term, and the bills of those reductions. The explicit experiment terms `ctGapTerm` and `ctCollTerm` are probabilities of the production source game as well (`ctGapTerm_source`, `ctCollTerm_source`). The production composition moves only the probabilities of the real experiment; it does not re-derive any bound.

The source programs that the source games run are reviewed transcriptions of the Rust adapter (RT in the ledger); the extracted core that they call is the Aeneas extraction. The equality of the source games with the CE and LQ-F games is proved (PB-D), but the step from Rust to the source programs is review, and every RT and E entry of the ledger stays as recorded there. The composition is fixed at codec′ and route′ (lane decision D-2): a headline at another codec would need a new reader-agreement fact, which is an owner-level decision. The abstract codec, its read-back laws and the shared frame reader stay E (Cap'n Proto is out of scope); `reader` and `erased` are universally quantified parameters of the production forms.

Not claimed: anything about the persistence-wrapped Server surface (at central every C, Go and Python Server handle runs through the persistence wrapper, which is outside the theorem boundary by owner decision; the Beacon surface of the bindings maps onto the experiment's commands); any later-quantum origin, UKS, binding or authentication statement (the later mode stays confidentiality only, because CE-12 has none); the closure of `ctGapTerm` (D-ct, a separate lane not on central; the F-ct2 forms keep the term explicit and the owner flag on it stands); a designation-free form of the pin or plaintext fields, the root, and plaintext-linked UKS (as in CE-12 P4); running time (bills count coins and oracle calls); and any statement about other identity indices than 0, a varying `checks`, or the Rust code beyond the reviewed transcription.

## Scans and hygiene

The production modules are composition modules: their statements restate frozen CE-12 statements, so the references that the item 7 contamination scans look for (item 2 risk 9 names and the PB exclusions, with the eleven rule classes of scan specification version 7) occur in them exactly where the frozen statements have them. The scans were therefore run to show that the production side carries none and that every remaining reference is inherited; the `.crypto` allowance of decision D-1 was not extended to these modules.

- Statement scan (exact-token scan of specification v7, `item7/prod/scripts/stmt_scan.py`, a copy of the I5 tool): the transfer module and the final module have no hit; the classical module has 7 (`PqxdhDeploymentIdealSteps.idealGame`, in the bounds of the seven CE-KT forms) and the later module 3 (`PqxdhRecordLiftLater.run`, in the bounds of the three CE-KT-L World forms); the liveness runs classify every witness token.
- Production-side statement scan (`item7/prod/scripts/stmt_split.py`): each production statement is split into its production side (every `𝒟[…]` block that contains a source game: the event read back through `unwrap`, the source game and its run) and the rest. On all four modules the production side has no hit, every block contains exactly one source game and no CE or LQ-F game, and every hit of the rest (the 7 and 3 above) occurs in the cited frozen statement. The production sides contain the token `setup.source.crypto` (47, 40 and 61 occurrences in the three headline modules): it is the CE events' own argument (Ed25519 verification of transcript values, as in the frozen events). Liveness: a fixture production-side block naming one witness per rule class is caught in all eleven classes, a fixture with an uninherited bound token fails, and the negative control does not hit.
- Direct-reference scan (Lean, specification v7, `item7/prod/scripts/direct_scan.py`, a copy of the I5 tool): the transfer module passes (8 declarations, no reference, no `.crypto` site) and its liveness run fails as intended. The three headline modules fail it, as restated CE-12 statements must, and the result is recorded: the classical module has 88 references (the projection `FixedHkdfSha512NoSaltSource.crypto` in 44 declarations, `PqxdhChallengeSecretRouting.FunctionalDecapsulation` in 37, `PqxdhDeploymentIdealSteps.idealGame` in 7), the later module 71 (the projection 35, `FunctionalDecapsulation` 33, `PqxdhRecordLiftLater.run` 3) and the final module 99 (the projection 50, `FunctionalDecapsulation` 49), and the `.crypto` site gate fails because these modules have no site table.
- Differential scan (Lean, `item7/prod/scripts/diff_scan.py`), which explains that result: for every production theorem, every rule-class constant of its type occurs among those of the frozen theorem that its proof cites (for the three ciphertext-collision forms also of the body of `ctCollTerm`, and for the two term lemmas of the term's definition), every `.crypto` site of its type is a site of that reference (all sites have the projected argument `Setup.source`: the `crypto` argument of the CE events, claims and designation and eligibility functions, of `Pqxdh.Crypto.verify` in the short-input and signature-adapter hypotheses and of `X25519ClassLaw` and `X25519ScalarSpread`, as in the frozen statements), its proof value adds no rule-class constant and no `.crypto` site, and the value cites exactly that one frozen theorem. So the references of the production modules are those of CE-12's bounds and premises (`FunctionalDecapsulation` is the approved KEM contract, `idealGame` the CE ideal game of CE-KT, `PqxdhRecordLiftLater.run` the later record world of the CE-KT-L World forms) and of its events.
- A text scan finds no constant of `PqxdhDeploymentProvenance`, no `authenticated_*` name, no `PqxdhRegistrationJointAuthenticatedSelectedKem` and no `PqxdhRegistrationJointKemKeyTransfer*` name in the four modules.
