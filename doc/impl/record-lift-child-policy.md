# Adjacent chain policy for the persistent evolving child

`PqxdhRecordLiftChildPolicy` identifies the persistent child response of `PqxdhRecordLiftChildRelay` with the next concrete mixed-policy hybrid. Fix a selected parent coordinate at which the lookup policy `ideal` is ideal, whose structural successor `advance parent 1` is still real, and a masked global state at that parent whose successor entry is still missing from the table. Running the original masked continuation with one uniformly drawn full 76-byte child then has the same law as running the actual concrete source interpreter under the adjacent policy `Function.update ideal (advance parent 1) true`, which makes the successor ideal as well, from the same state with the hidden parent restored. The comparison projects away only the private native and uniform analysis counters. The shared ROM cache, effect counts, lazy seed cache, the complete chain table, the produced and pending frame logs, every typed reply including optional failures, and the complete client result are retained.

## Single-service facts

`reads_iff` shows that, given the source availability lower bound `start direction ≤ position` on the parent, the only coordinate other than the parent that structurally reads it (`MaskedReads.Reads`) is its successor; the total natural-number fallback at position zero is handled explicitly. `needs_iff` turns this into the relay's request condition: at a ready state, `ChildRelay.Needs` holds exactly for the lookup of the successor. `coin_lazy` shows that forwarding an actual uniform computation neither queries nor changes the persistent child cell.

The state view `view hidden` is `CtxProtocol.erasePrivate ∘ GlobalMask.realize hidden`: it restores the hidden parent value, on the proof side only, and removes the native and uniform counters. `other_source` and `different_source` show that every service other than the successor lookup has the same law after this view as the adjacent policy's bare source interpreter. They need no byte inequality and no successful-reply condition. `lookup_child` shows that the actual real successor lookup overwrites its own cached response with the supplied full 76-byte value and charges its native KDF call. `relay_child` shows that the relay samples once at the first real successor read and reuses the stored value afterwards, while the source still executes and charges its own lookup each time. `view_putChild` and `view_child` show that the view commutes with that cache write and that the cell relation `CellMatches` tracks the literal successor table entry, not an independent sample. `child_source` combines these: at the successor, the relay and the adjacent ideal lookup give the same counter-erased source state, both for a first miss and for a repeated hit.

`relay_different`, `query_source`, `different_entry` and `query_invariant` complete the single-step picture. Every private service of the lazy-child relay agrees with the adjacent policy on the counter-erased state. Services other than the successor lookup preserve the persistent successor entry, including failed native operations and unrelated random lookups. The invariant `Good` (the masked coordinate is the fixed parent, `Ready`, and `CellMatches`) is preserved under every supported reply.

## Whole-execution laws

`step_good` lifts the invariant to the flattened relay interpreter, and `schedule_source` proves that, under `Good`, the entire adaptive relay with its extra cell equals the adjacent bare source interpreter after the view. `flatten_run` shows that flattening the extra child-cell transformer preserves the complete execution literally, before any projection. `independent_source` then combines `ChildRelay.deferred_source`, `flatten_run` and `schedule_source`: drawing the child up front and running the masked interpreter has the adjacent bare law under every subsequent probabilistic observation of the result and the complete counter-erased state (`LazyOracleDiscard.LawEq`), not only equal marginal distributions.

`independent_observed` states the same law at the observer boundary of `PqxdhRecordLiftGlobalCut`. Its left side is the independent-child continuation that `GlobalCut.parentObserver` runs on a reached cut, followed by `GlobalCut.observeMasked`. Its right side is the actual `Protocol.interpreter` under the adjacent policy, run from `GlobalMask.realize hidden state` and followed by `GlobalCut.observe`. Both observers receive the complete client result, the shared ROM cache and both frame logs, and nothing else.

## Scope and accounting

The premise `Good parent ideal (state, none)` is operational: the state's masked coordinate is `parent`, the parent satisfies the availability lower bound, the policy is ideal at the parent and real at the successor, and the successor entry is missing. This module does not derive it at the sampled cut; the hybrid step that composes `GlobalCut.observed_bound`, `PqxdhRecordLiftPrefixPolicy` and this module still has to discharge it there from `SourcePrefix` and `Availability`.

The view erases only the private native and uniform counters. The relay's child draw is outside the original uniform ledger, and the adjacent policy's ideal draw at the successor is counted by its own interpreter but erased by the view; the enclosing bills must count this draw. `other_source` and `different_source` instantiate `ChildRelay.unused_source` with the real production HKDF stream at the ratchet address of the hidden parent value, the same real derivation that the published `GlobalMask` (in `GlobalMask.interpreter_realization`) and `GlobalCut` use; `ChildRelay.unused_source` itself is parametric in the child value. No random table replaces a public HKDF evaluation.

All nineteen statements are operational equalities, invariants or contextual distribution laws. They add no cryptographic contract, no source reachability assumption, no byte inequality and no oracle-security premise. The module does not prove the hybrid step itself, the seed-mask analogue for the initial child, alias or refusal bounds, query bills or any record security theorem.
