# Shared-cache adaptive CTX ciphertext privacy

This stage connects the actual independent-key shared-cache CTX family to a single explicit ciphertext-randomized experiment. It reuses the previously checked modified nonce-AEAD and CTX integrity components; no primitive algorithm or implementation is proved. Every source key is sampled independently and uniformly as 32 bytes in original index order. Coincident values are retained, and no key-injectivity or cross-key nonce-inequality premise is added. The source adversary receives indexed sealing, arbitrary raw receive, public hashing and uniform queries with their original typed replies. Private complete-state equations do not expose the sampled keys, histories or caches through additional adversary capabilities.

The original all-real shared-cache CTX source first moves through the already published ordered integrity/replay hybrids. Its all-replay endpoint is then factored, with complete state, into a key-free compiler over an indexed family of ordinary nonce-respecting AEAD encryption oracles. Only after that exact equality does a second ordered family replace those ordinary encryption replies by independently random ciphertext bodies and sixteen-byte tags, preserving the same nonce gates and histories. The compiler retains one common public CTX cache across every index and a consistent private suffix dictionary at each index. It never recovers a hidden plaintext to insert into an adversary-supplied request.

## The actual ordinary family and selected reduction

`AdaptiveSealKeySelection` keeps every original source index. `selected_step` and `selected_schedule` reconstruct the entire original array after selected or unselected updates, retaining every nonce and successful seal entry. `fixed_source_selection` establishes the exact empty initial histories. `prepared_run` interprets the actual other-key sampler while retaining the target state. `source_prepared_selection` then applies the existing finite key partition under the complete evaluated continuation. The source's original finite sampler and challenger-first selected sampler therefore agree with all retained states, rather than merely having equal marginal key distributions.

`AdaptiveSealMultiKeyPrivacy` constructs an ordinary Boolean IND$ adversary for each selected original index. `real_reduction` and `random_reduction` identify its actual real and random endpoint distributions. The source random hybrid still pays for every source key draw; the selected unused key is eliminated only in the evaluated random distribution used to match the ordinary endpoint, which has no key-generation step. `selected_advantage` is an exact equality to that constructed ordinary advantage. `hop_advantage` identifies the adjacent original-index family flags. `family_bound` invokes the finite hybrid inequality only after those adjacent equalities, and `all_keys_bound` exposes the actual all-real and all-random indexed games.

There is one reduction per original key. The bound is the sum of their advantages, so a common ordinary bound epsilon gives K times epsilon for K keys. This stage does not multiply by a second random target guess. If two indices happen to have equal key bytes, both values remain in the exact simulations and in the constructed ordinary games; the proof does not silently condition on their distinctness.

## Complete ordinary source and reduction accounting

`AdaptiveSealKeyBounds` has twenty-three statements. `local_uniform_bound` charges one draw for each explicit uniform request, zero uniform draws for deterministic real sealing, and at most the submitted plaintext length plus sixteen draws for random sealing. Used-nonce rejection can reduce that cost, but every attempt remains in the source budget.

`reduction_seal_step`, `wrapped_seal_bound`, `selection_step_bound`, `selection_bound` and `prepared_seal_bound` preserve the exact selected-key sealing cap through the local reduction, original-index routing and other-key preparation. Other keys execute locally and make no selected-challenger encryption call; that fact does not make their cryptographic computation or randomized ciphertext generation free.

`work_simulate` transfers an explicit byte-weighted per-query cost through the full stateful adaptive continuation. `reduction_uniform_step`, `wrapped_uniform_bound`, `selected_work_bound` and `prepared_uniform_bound` apply it to the actual reduction. Its exact cost charges all explicit uniform queries, zero for a selected seal forwarded to the challenger, zero random draws for other real seals, and plaintext length plus sixteen for every other random seal. The weighted predicate quantifies over all possible typed replies and continuations. Thus, with N other keys and a bound W on that displayed cost, the reduction makes at most 32*N+W uniform queries.

`reduction_plaintext_step`, `wrapped_plaintext_bound` and `prepared_plaintext_bound` separately preserve the submitted plaintext length of each selected seal. Other-key local encryption contributes no selected-challenger plaintext bytes, while its actual randomized generation is already charged to W. `reduction_bounds` combines selected seal cap S, selected plaintext cap B, and uniform cap 32*N+W for the exact constructed Boolean adversary. `experiment_bounds` charges the complete ordinary experiments: real costs at most 32+(32*N+W) uniform draws, and random costs at most 32*N+W+B+16*S. The random endpoint has no encryption-key draw. No validation seal is added in this ciphertext-view reduction.

`work_mono`, `work_of_global_caps`, `selected_uniform_cost_bound` and `prepared_global_uniform_bound` derive the conservative bound W <= U+B_all+16*S_all from the global indexed source coin, total submitted plaintext and total sealing caps. The exact mode-aware cost can be smaller and remains available. `family_uniform_step`, `family_run_uniform_bound` and `source_uniform_bound` independently account for the actual source family: 32*K plus its displayed mode-aware cost, including keys generated for modes that do not subsequently use them. Equality of evaluated distributions is never used to discard actual source sampling costs.

These are oracle and byte-draw bounds. Local AEAD evaluation, nonce/history comparison, ciphertext and plaintext copying, parsing, cache scans and updates, and index reconstruction remain actual computation bounded by the corresponding submitted traffic and lengths. They are not declared free or silently replaced by an uncharged ambient oracle. A concrete running-time estimate must include those local operations.

## Combined CTX statement

`AdaptiveCtxSharedMultiKeyPrivacy.privacyReduction` is the explicit ordinary privacy adversary obtained by applying the selected-key reduction to the actual key-free CTX compiler. `family_bound` composes the published all-key common-cache integrity bound with that compiler's privacy bound by a triangle inequality at the exact same all-replay experiment. `global_family_bound` derives the original selected integrity caps from the global public, seal and raw caps before applying the same composition.

For each original key j, let h_j bound the routed public-hash service, s_j bound its selected seal calls and r_j bound its selected raw attempts, with s_j < 2^96. The contribution is the ordinary IND$ advantage of the existing validation reduction, h_j/2^128, twice the sum of the r_j constructed ordinary INT-CTXT forgery advantages, r_j/2^512, and the ordinary IND$ advantage of the new ciphertext-view reduction. The final bound sums these contributions over all original indices. Every adaptive verification attempt is counted, including failed parsing/lookup and multiple accepted attempts as specified by the underlying raw interface. The conservative global integrity caps are h_j <= H+S+R, s_j <= S and r_j <= R. The earlier exact mode-aware integrity query and byte ledgers remain unchanged.

The final random view is an actual program: the same CTX compiler, source indices, parser, private suffix dictionaries, common public cache, nonce gates and raw-reply logic interpreted with the all-random ordinary sealing family. It is not a newly assumed protocol-security endpoint or a list of primitive names.

## Scope and remaining connection

The random-oracle contract here is a programmable classical oracle for the modeled BLAKE2b/modified-CTX use. Collision resistance alone does not imply that contract. Ordinary nonce-AEAD IND$ and INT-CTXT remain the stated primitive security endpoints; implementations, compiler and runtime behavior retain their documented contracts.

The source key distribution in this stage is independently uniform keys. These equations do not prove that production evolving ratchet materials have that distribution, that repeated HKDF projections are independent, or that each source position can be replaced by a fresh key. Native receive consumes successful keys and rolls back failed attempts through the separately checked source owner; the generic raw component itself does not replace those source transition proofs. Current-chain reveals expose descendants, and restored/copied owners may repeat material. Those actual correlations, freshness/reveal rules and deployment ownership assumptions must be connected in the common protocol experiment before this component supports a production secrecy or authentication event. Public fixed HKDF evaluations stay real in that later composition; the distinguishable public-HKDF/random-table endpoint is not used here.
